IAS 28 — Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures

Indian equivalent: Ind AS 28 — Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures

Objective and scope

IAS 28 explains how to account for investments where you have significant influence but not control — an associate — and for joint ventures, using the equity method, which sits between simply holding an investment and fully consolidating a subsidiary.

Core principle

If you own enough of another company to influence it but not control it (typically 20–50%), you don't consolidate it line-by-line, but you can't ignore its performance either. The equity method is the middle path: start with cost, then increase or decrease your investment by your share of the associate's profits, losses and dividends.

Defined terms and technical terminology

Term

Technical meaning

Associate

An entity over which the investor has significant influence — usually 20% to 50% of voting power.

Significant influence

The power to participate in financial and operating policy decisions — but not control them.

Joint venture

A joint arrangement where parties with joint control have rights to the net assets of the arrangement.

Equity method

Investment recorded at cost, then adjusted for the investor's share of the investee's post-acquisition profits/losses; dividends reduce the carrying amount.

Application and worked illustrations

The equity method mechanics

Under the equity method you record the investment initially at cost. Each period you add your share of the associate's profit (which also goes to your own profit) and subtract your share of its losses. When the associate pays you a dividend, you reduce the investment carrying amount (the dividend is a return of value already recognised, not new income). Your share of the associate's OCI is also picked up in your OCI.

Worked example

InvestCo buys 30% of AssocCo for ₹50,00,000 — enough for significant influence, so AssocCo is an associate. In the year, AssocCo earns a profit of ₹20,00,000 and pays total dividends of ₹5,00,000. InvestCo's share of profit = 30% × ₹20,00,000 = ₹6,00,000 (added to its investment and its profit). Its share of dividends = 30% × ₹5,00,000 = ₹1,50,000 (reduces the investment). Closing carrying amount = 50,00,000 + 6,00,000 − 1,50,000 = ₹54,50,000.

When losses exceed the investment

If your share of losses reduces the investment to zero, you generally stop recognising further losses (unless you have guaranteed the associate's obligations), because you can't lose more than you put in.

Common mistake: recording dividends from an associate as income. Under the equity method the dividend reduces the investment — the income was already recognised via your share of profit.

IND AS LINK

Ind AS 28 aligns with IAS 28 — the equity method for associates (significant influence) and joint ventures (joint control), consistent with Ind AS 111 on joint arrangements.

Further worked illustrations

Extra numeric example — associate makes a loss

InvestCo owns 25% of AssocCo (carrying amount ₹30,00,000). This year AssocCo makes a loss of ₹40,00,000. InvestCo's share = 25% × ₹40,00,000 = ₹10,00,000, reducing the investment to ₹20,00,000 and reducing InvestCo's own profit by ₹10,00,000. The equity method captures the bad news as faithfully as the good.

INDIAN REGULATORY CONTEXT

Many Indian infrastructure and telecom projects are run as joint ventures. Under Ind AS 28, the venturer uses the equity method — a change from older Indian GAAP (AS 27), which allowed proportionate consolidation. So a company that once added its share of each JV asset and liability line-by-line now shows a single 'investment in JV' line.

TECHNICAL EDGE CASE

If InvestCo's share of losses would push the investment below zero, it stops recognising further losses once the carrying amount hits nil (and any long-term interest, like loans to the associate, is written down first). It doesn't keep booking losses — unless it has a legal or constructive obligation to fund the associate. Unrecognised losses are tracked and offset against future profits before the investment starts rising again.
