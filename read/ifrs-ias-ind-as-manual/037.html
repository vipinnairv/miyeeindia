Section 2 - Industry-Specific Application and VC/PE Mandates

The standards do not apply uniformly across sectors. A SaaS business lives and dies by IFRS 15 and IAS 38; a manufacturer by IAS 2, IAS 16 and IAS 36; a lender by IFRS 9. This section maps the standards that dominate each major industry, then examines two areas of disproportionate practical difficulty - share-based payments and derivative instruments - before turning to why venture capital and private equity investors routinely mandate IFRS or Ind AS adoption in their portfolio companies.

2.1 Industry Application Matrices

2.1.1 Software as a Service (SaaS) and Technology

The SaaS model concentrates almost all its accounting risk in revenue recognition and capitalisation judgements. Subscription arrangements bundle licences, hosting, implementation, support and professional services, and the analysis of whether these are distinct performance obligations determines both the amount and the timing of revenue.

The IFRIC agenda decision (March 2021) concluded that costs of configuring or customising a supplier's application software in a SaaS arrangement are generally expensed as incurred, because the customer does not control a software asset. This reversed prevailing practice at many entities and remains a live audit issue.

INDIAN REGULATORY CONTEXT

NBFCs applying Ind AS must reconcile Ind AS 109 ECL with RBI's IRACP norms. Where IRACP provisioning exceeds Ind AS ECL, the shortfall is appropriated to an 'Impairment Reserve' within equity, and cannot be reversed to profit. Separately, Ind AS implementation for scheduled commercial banks remains deferred by the RBI, creating a two-tier reporting landscape in Indian financial services.

2.2 Deep Dive - Accounting for ESOPs under IFRS 2 / Ind AS 102

Employee stock options are the defining compensation instrument of high-growth companies, and their accounting is consistently misunderstood by founders and finance teams. The central proposition of IFRS 2 is that the issue of equity instruments in exchange for services is a transaction with economic cost, notwithstanding the absence of any cash outflow.

Valuation inputs

Because employee options are rarely traded, fair value is determined using an option pricing model - typically Black-Scholes-Merton for options with only service conditions, or a binomial/lattice model or Monte Carlo simulation where market conditions, early exercise behaviour or path dependency exist.

PRACTICE NOTE

For unlisted Indian startups, the two most contentious inputs are share price - requiring an independent valuation, often a recent priced funding round adjusted for preference-share rights - and expected volatility, which must be proxied from listed comparables because no trading history exists. Both are areas of significant auditor challenge.

The recognition pattern - graded vesting

Where an award vests in tranches (graded vesting), each tranche is treated as a separate award with its own vesting period and fair value. This produces an accelerated expense profile, because the first tranche is expensed over one year, the second over two, and so on, with all tranches overlapping in the early periods.

TECHNICAL EDGE CASE

Where an entity withholds shares to settle the employee's tax obligation (a 'net settlement' feature), IFRS 2 provides an exception: the entire award is classified as equity-settled, notwithstanding that the withheld portion is settled in cash to the tax authority. Without this exception, the award would be bifurcated, producing significant and volatile P&L remeasurement.

2.3 Deep Dive - Derivative Instruments and Hedge Accounting

A derivative is a financial instrument with all three of the following characteristics: its value changes in response to an underlying; it requires no or minimal initial net investment; and it is settled at a future date. All derivatives are measured at fair value through profit or loss, unless designated in a qualifying hedge relationship.

Why hedge accounting exists

Consider an entity with a highly probable forecast US dollar purchase in six months, hedged with a forward contract. Absent hedge accounting, the derivative is remeasured through profit or loss each period, while the forecast transaction is not yet recognised at all. The result is accounting mismatch: reported earnings swing on a hedge that is, economically, reducing risk. Hedge accounting exists solely to eliminate this artificial volatility.

Qualifying criteria under IFRS 9

IFRS 9 removed the IAS 39 80–125% bright-line effectiveness test in favour of a principles-based assessment. A hedging relationship qualifies where: (a) it comprises eligible hedging instruments and hedged items; (b) there is formal designation and documentation at inception of the relationship, the risk management objective and strategy; and (c) it meets all three effectiveness requirements - an economic relationship between item and instrument, credit risk does not dominate the value changes, and the hedge ratio reflects the quantities actually used to hedge.

TECHNICAL EDGE CASE

Where a hedged forecast transaction ceases to be highly probable, hedge accounting is discontinued prospectively, but the amount accumulated in the cash flow hedge reserve remains in OCI until the transaction occurs. However, if the forecast transaction is no longer expected to occur at all, the accumulated balance must be immediately reclassified to profit or loss. This distinction - unlikely versus not expected - determines whether a deferred loss crystallises in the current period.

INDIAN REGULATORY CONTEXT

Corporates with foreign-currency borrowings and importers routinely hedge with forwards and options. Under Ind AS 109, documentation discipline at inception is the single most common failure point - where documentation is deficient or retrospective, hedge accounting is denied entirely and the full derivative fair value movement is recognised in profit or loss, producing precisely the earnings volatility the treasury function sought to avoid.

2.4 Why VC and PE Investors Mandate IFRS / Ind AS Adoption

Venture capital and private equity investors routinely require portfolio companies to adopt IFRS or Ind AS, frequently as a condition precedent in the share subscription and shareholders' agreement. The rationale is not academic; it is driven by valuation, monitoring and exit mechanics.

2.4.1 Valuation integrity and comparability

Investment theses are built on multiples - EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E. Each of these is only as reliable as the underlying accounting. Three specific concerns dominate:

Revenue quality. IFRS 15 prevents the upfront recognition of multi-year subscription contracts and forces gross-versus-net (principal-agent) analysis. A marketplace recognising gross merchandise value as revenue rather than net commission can overstate its top line by an order of magnitude - and an investor paying an EV/Revenue multiple on that figure is materially overpaying. IFRS 15 adoption converts reported revenue into a defensible basis for valuation.

EBITDA integrity. IFRS 2 forces recognition of ESOP cost, and IAS 38 restricts capitalisation of development expenditure. Aggressive capitalisation of software development or customer acquisition costs inflates EBITDA and understates cash burn - precisely the metrics that determine both valuation and runway. Conversely, IFRS 16 increases reported EBITDA by moving lease rentals below the line into depreciation and interest, which investors must normalise when comparing pre- and post-adoption periods.

Balance sheet completeness. IFRS 9 ECL provisioning, IAS 19 actuarial valuation of gratuity, IAS 37 provisions and IFRS 16 lease liabilities collectively surface obligations that cash-basis or older-GAAP accounts leave invisible. For a PE buyer computing enterprise value to equity value bridges, undisclosed liabilities translate directly into purchase price adjustments.

PRACTICE NOTE

Sophisticated facility agreements address this through a 'frozen GAAP' or 'GAAP reset' clause: financial covenants are tested under the accounting framework prevailing at signing, or the parties agree to renegotiate in good faith on a change in framework. Where such a clause is absent, IFRS 16 adoption alone has historically triggered technical covenant breaches requiring lender waivers.

2.4.3 Exit readiness - IPO, strategic sale and secondary

The exit rationale is the most compelling. An Indian company pursuing an IPO must present restated Ind AS financial statements for the periods required under the SEBI ICDR Regulations in its offer document. An entity that has been reporting under Indian GAAP faces a compressed, high-risk conversion exercise at precisely the moment when management bandwidth is scarcest and error is least tolerable. Early adoption converts a transaction-critical path item into routine business-as-usual reporting.

For a strategic sale to a multinational acquirer, the target's financials must be capable of consolidation into the acquirer's IFRS group accounts. A target already reporting under IFRS or Ind AS dramatically shortens due diligence, reduces the scope of the quality of earnings review, and narrows the range of purchase price adjustments the buyer can credibly demand.

For a secondary sale to another financial sponsor, audited IFRS statements provide the incoming investor with a comparable basis against its other portfolio holdings and its own fund reporting requirements.

VC/PE Mandate Rationale - Value Chain

■ INVESTMENT: Diligence on IFRS/Ind AS financials → reliable revenue, EBITDA and net debt for valuation

▼

▸ DOCUMENTATION: Condition precedent in SSA/SHA requiring IFRS/Ind AS adoption and Big 4 audit

INDIAN REGULATORY CONTEXT

Ind AS applies mandatorily to listed companies and to unlisted companies with net worth of ₹250 crore or more (with holding, subsidiary, associate and JV companies of covered entities also drawn in). Companies below the threshold apply AS (Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2021). Critically, voluntary adoption of Ind AS is irreversible - once adopted, a company cannot revert to AS. This makes the VC/PE mandate a genuinely strategic commitment rather than a reversible reporting preference, and it should be negotiated with that permanence in mind.
