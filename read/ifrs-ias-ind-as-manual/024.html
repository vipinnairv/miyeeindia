IFRS 3 — Business Combinations

Indian equivalent: Ind AS 103 — Business Combinations

Objective and scope

IFRS 3 explains how to account when one company acquires control of another business — how to identify the acquirer, measure everything acquired at fair value, and calculate and record goodwill (or a bargain-purchase gain).

Core principle

When you buy a whole business you rarely pay exactly the value of its net assets — you usually pay more, for its reputation, customers, staff and synergies. IFRS 3 requires the acquisition method: measure the identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value, and the extra you paid becomes goodwill.

Defined terms and technical terminology

Term

Technical meaning

Business combination

A transaction in which an acquirer obtains control of one or more businesses.

Acquirer

The entity that obtains control.

Acquisition method

The required technique: recognise the acquiree's identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value at acquisition date.

Goodwill

Consideration transferred + NCI + fair value of any previously held interest − fair value of net identifiable assets acquired. Captures reputation, synergies, workforce.

Bargain purchase

Where the net assets acquired exceed the price paid — the excess is a gain in profit (after re-checking).

Non-controlling interest (NCI)

The portion of a subsidiary not owned by the parent.

Consideration transferred

The fair value of what the acquirer gives — cash, shares, or assets.

Application and worked illustrations

The acquisition method, step by step

(1) Identify the acquirer — the party gaining control. (2) Determine the acquisition date. (3) Recognise and measure the identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value — including some the acquiree never recorded, such as internally generated brands or customer relationships. (4) Recognise goodwill or a bargain-purchase gain.

Worked example 1 — goodwill

BigCo buys SmallCo for ₹10 crore cash. On fair-value assessment, SmallCo's identifiable assets are ₹12 crore and liabilities ₹5 crore → net identifiable assets ₹7 crore. Goodwill = ₹10 crore − ₹7 crore = ₹3 crore, recorded on the consolidated balance sheet. Goodwill is not amortised but tested for impairment annually (IAS 36).

Worked example 2 — bargain purchase

If BigCo had paid only ₹6 crore for those ₹7 crore of net assets — perhaps SmallCo was a distressed seller — the ₹1 crore excess is a bargain-purchase gain, recognised immediately in profit (only after re-verifying that all assets and liabilities were correctly identified and valued).

Non-controlling interest

If BigCo bought only 80% of SmallCo, the other 20% is non-controlling interest, measured either at fair value (full goodwill method) or at its proportionate share of net assets (partial goodwill method) — an accounting-policy choice per transaction.

Common mistake: treating acquisition-related costs (legal, advisory fees) as part of the cost. Under IFRS 3 these are expensed, not capitalised into goodwill.

IND AS LINK

Ind AS 103 follows IFRS 3's acquisition method, but transactions under common control (mergers within the same group) use the pooling-of-interests method under Appendix C — assets/liabilities carried at book value, no fair-value step-up, no new goodwill. This is a key India-specific feature.

Further worked illustrations

Extra numeric example — fair-valuing an unrecorded intangible

BigCo acquires TargetCo. TargetCo never recorded its valuable customer relationships, but at acquisition they're independently valued at ₹5 crore. IFRS 3 requires BigCo to recognise that ₹5 crore intangible as a separate identifiable asset (reducing goodwill by ₹5 crore), even though it was invisible on TargetCo's own books. Fair-valuing previously-unrecorded intangibles is a core part of purchase price allocation.

INDIAN REGULATORY CONTEXT

A very common Indian scenario: a group reorganises by merging two of its own subsidiaries. Under Ind AS 103 Appendix C, this common-control combination uses the pooling-of-interests method — assets and liabilities carried at existing book values, no fair-value step-up, and no new goodwill. This deliberately differs from full IFRS, which has no special common-control method.

TECHNICAL EDGE CASE

BigCo agrees to pay TargetCo's owners an extra ₹2 crore if profits hit a target next year. This contingent consideration is measured at fair value and included in the cost at acquisition. Later changes in the estimate are usually taken to profit or loss (if the contingent payment is a liability), not adjusted against goodwill — a point often gotten wrong.

Extended technical analysis

The acquisition method — comprehensive mechanics

IFRS 3 requires the acquisition method for every business combination. Its technical difficulty concentrates in four areas: determining whether the acquired set constitutes a business; identifying the acquirer; measuring consideration transferred including contingent consideration; and recognising identifiable net assets at fair value, with the residual being goodwill.

Step 1 — Is it a business?

A business requires, at minimum, an input and a substantive process that together contribute to the ability to create outputs. The concentration test (an optional screen introduced by the 2018 amendments) provides that if substantially all of the fair value of gross assets acquired is concentrated in a single identifiable asset or group of similar assets, the acquisition is not a business and is accounted for as an asset acquisition — with no goodwill, no deferred tax on initial recognition, and transaction costs capitalised rather than expensed. This distinction has significant consequences.

Identifiable net assets — recognition beyond the acquiree's books

The acquirer recognises identifiable assets and liabilities regardless of whether the acquiree had recognised them. This routinely brings previously unrecognised intangibles onto the balance sheet — customer relationships, order backlog, trade names, technology, non-compete agreements — because they meet the separability or contractual-legal criterion. Conversely, the acquirer must not recognise the acquiree's internally generated goodwill, future restructuring costs it is not obliged to incur, or expected synergies as separate assets.

Non-controlling interests — the two measurement choices

For each transaction separately, the acquirer elects to measure NCI at either fair value (the full goodwill method, recognising goodwill attributable to both the parent and the NCI) or at the NCI's proportionate share of identifiable net assets (the partial goodwill method). The election materially changes reported goodwill and NCI, and consequently the amount at risk on subsequent impairment testing.

Measurement period and subsequent accounting

Where the initial accounting is incomplete, the acquirer reports provisional amounts and has a measurement period — not exceeding twelve months from the acquisition date — to retrospectively adjust them for facts and circumstances that existed at the acquisition date. Adjustments after that window, or arising from post-acquisition events, are not goodwill adjustments; they go to profit or loss. Subsequently, goodwill is not amortised but tested for impairment annually under IAS 36, and impairment of goodwill can never be reversed.

PRACTICE NOTE

— advisory, legal, valuation and other professional fees — are expensed as incurred and are not part of consideration transferred (IFRS 3.53). Costs of issuing debt or equity, however, are accounted for under IFRS 9 and IAS 32 respectively.

IFRS vs Ind AS — Comparative Analysis

Aspect

IFRS / IAS position

Ind AS position (carve-out / carve-in)

Method

Acquisition method for all business combinations.

Ind AS 103 — converged for combinations not under common control.

Common control transactions

No specific IFRS guidance; entities develop a policy.

Major carve-in: Appendix C to Ind AS 103 mandates the pooling of interests method — assets and liabilities at carrying amounts, no fair value step-up, no goodwill, and the difference taken to capital reserve. Comparatives restated from the beginning of the earliest period presented.

Bargain purchase gain

Recognised in profit or loss after reassessment.

Carve-out: recognised in Other Comprehensive Income and accumulated in capital reserve; if there is no clear evidence of the underlying reason, it is recognised directly in capital reserve — not in P&L.

NCI measurement

Same choice available.

Reverse acquisitions
