24.1 QRMP scheme

Taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to Rs. 5 crore may opt for the Quarterly Return Monthly Payment scheme. They file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly but pay tax monthly in PMT-06 by the 25th for the first two months, either by the fixed sum method (35% of the cash tax of the previous quarter, or the full cash tax of the last month of that quarter for a monthly filer) or by self-assessment. B2B invoices of the first two months may go through IFF (up to Rs. 50 lakh a month) so customers get monthly credit. Quarterly GSTR-3B is due on the 22nd in States such as Gujarat, Maharashtra and Karnataka, and on the 24th in others such as Uttar Pradesh and Delhi.

24.2 GSTR-1 and GSTR-1A

GSTR-1 reports B2B supplies invoice-wise (Table 4), inter-State B2C invoices above Rs. 1 lakh (Table 5), exports and SEZ supplies (Table 6), other B2C supplies in summary (Table 7), nil, exempt and non-GST supplies (Table 8), amendments and credit and debit notes (Tables 9 and 10), advances (Table 11), the HSN summary (Table 12, split B2B and B2C), documents issued (Table 13) and e-commerce supplies (Tables 14 and 15). GSTR-1A (from the July 2024 period) lets a taxpayer add or correct records of the same period before filing GSTR-3B.

Rule 59(6). A person cannot file GSTR-1 if GSTR-3B for the preceding tax period (month or quarter) is not filed. GSTR-1 is also blocked where an intimation under rule 88C (liability gap between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B) or rule 88D (ITC in GSTR-3B more than GSTR-2B) is pending without payment or explanation. Customers then lose credit.

Judicial view: Correction of returns

Union of India v. Bharti Airtel Ltd. (Supreme Court, 2021): The taxpayer wanted to rectify GSTR-3B for July to September 2017 by reducing cash paid on the ground that credit was not visible then. The Supreme Court held there is no right to rectify GSTR-3B outside section 39(9); correction is made in the period the error is noticed, within time.

Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs v. Aberdare Technologies (P) Ltd. (Supreme Court, 2025): The Bombay High Court (2024) had allowed correction of bona fide clerical errors in GSTR-1 after the time limit, where there was no loss of revenue, since the customer's credit depended on the correction. The Supreme Court dismissed the department's appeal: the right to correct clerical errors flows from the right to carry on business, and portal limits cannot defeat it.

Practical relevance: A genuine clerical error (wrong GSTIN, B2B shown as B2C) with no revenue loss can be pursued by representation and, if refused, by writ. Deliberate re-computation of liability is governed by Bharti Airtel.

24.4 Three-year time bar

The Finance Act, 2023 amended sections 37, 39, 44, 45 and 52 so that a return cannot be filed after three years from its due date. The portal enforces this from the July 2025 period. A barred return leaves the taxpayer open to best judgment assessment (section 62) and customers without credit.

Illustration 135: Last date under the three-year bar

Rana Enterprises did not file its GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B for August 2023 or its GSTR-9 for FY 2022-23.

Step 1: GSTR-1 for August 2023 was due on 11 September 2023; last date 11 September 2026 (already barred on 28 September 2026).

Step 2: GSTR-3B for August 2023 was due on 20 September 2023; last date 20 September 2026 (barred).

Step 3: GSTR-9 for FY 2022-23 was due on 31 December 2023; last date 31 December 2026 (still open).

24.5 Late fee and interest

Late fee under section 47 for GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B is Rs. 50 per day (Rs. 25 CGST + Rs. 25 SGST), and Rs. 20 per day for nil returns, subject to caps: Rs. 500 for nil returns; Rs. 2,000 for turnover up to Rs. 1.5 crore; Rs. 5,000 for turnover from Rs. 1.5 crore to Rs. 5 crore; Rs. 10,000 above Rs. 5 crore (all amounts combined). Interest at 18% under section 50(1) applies on tax paid late in cash. Nil GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and CMP-08 can be filed by SMS from the registered mobile to 14409.

Illustration 136: Late fee and interest on a delayed GSTR-3B

Lakhani Foods, with turnover of Rs. 3 crore, files GSTR-3B 45 days late. Net tax payable in cash is Rs. 2,40,000. Its sister concern files a nil return 30 days late.

Step 1: Late fee = Rs. 50 x 45 = Rs. 2,250 (CGST Rs. 1,125 + SGST Rs. 1,125); below the Rs. 5,000 cap.

Step 2: Interest = Rs. 2,40,000 x 18% x 45 / 365 = Rs. 5,326.

Step 3: Sister concern: Rs. 20 x 30 = Rs. 600, capped at Rs. 500.

24.6 Annual return, final return and GST practitioners

GSTR-9 is optional for taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to Rs. 2 crore (made permanent from FY 2024-25). GSTR-9C, a self-certified reconciliation of the annual return with audited financial statements, is required where turnover exceeds Rs. 5 crore. Both are due on 31 December. Late fee for GSTR-9 is Rs. 50 per day (turnover up to Rs. 5 crore, cap 0.04% of turnover), Rs. 100 per day (Rs. 5 crore to 20 crore, cap 0.04%) and Rs. 200 per day (above Rs. 20 crore, cap 0.5%). GSTR-10 is the final return of a person whose registration is cancelled.

Illustration 137: GSTR-9 late fee

Kapoor Paints, turnover Rs. 8 crore, files GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26 sixty days late.

Step 1: Rate = Rs. 100 per day. Late fee = Rs. 100 x 60 = Rs. 6,000.

Step 2: Cap = 0.04% of Rs. 8 crore = Rs. 32,000. Late fee payable = Rs. 6,000.

A GST practitioner enrolled under section 48 and rule 83 (FORM PCT-01) may furnish returns and other details on a taxpayer's authorisation. The taxpayer remains responsible and confirms the return.

Chapter summary

• GSTR-1 by 11th, GSTR-3B by 20th; QRMP taxpayers file quarterly (13th and 22nd or 24th) and pay monthly by 25th.

• GSTR-1A allows corrections before GSTR-3B; from July 2025 Table 3 of GSTR-3B is locked.

• Returns cannot be filed after three years from the due date.

• Late fee Rs. 50 per day (Rs. 20 for nil), capped by turnover; interest at 18% on cash paid late.

• GSTR-9 is optional up to Rs. 2 crore; GSTR-9C above Rs. 5 crore; both by 31 December.

• Bona fide clerical errors may be corrected even after the time limit where no revenue is lost (Aberdare Technologies).
