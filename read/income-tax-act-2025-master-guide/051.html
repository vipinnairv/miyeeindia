48. Case Study 1: Salaried Taxpayer with Property and Investments

Facts. Anita, 45, a resident, works in Bengaluru. For Tax Year 2026-27 her details are:

  • Basic salary Rs. 12,00,000; HRA Rs. 4,80,000; special allowance Rs. 6,00,000; employer NPS contribution Rs. 1,20,000 (10% of basic). Professional tax Rs. 2,400.
  • She pays rent of Rs. 40,000 a month (Rs. 4,80,000 a year) for her flat in Bengaluru.
  • She owns a flat in Mysuru let out at Rs. 25,000 a month. Municipal tax paid Rs. 15,000. Home loan interest Rs. 3,50,000.
  • Savings bank interest Rs. 18,000; FD interest Rs. 1,20,000; dividends Rs. 40,000.
  • LTCG on listed shares Rs. 2,50,000; STCG on equity funds Rs. 60,000.
  • Old regime deductions available: section 123 Rs. 1,50,000; section 126 Rs. 75,000 (self Rs. 25,000, senior parents Rs. 50,000); section 124 own NPS Rs. 50,000.

Conclusion. The two regimes give almost the same tax; the old regime is lower by only Rs. 229. The addition of Bengaluru to the 50% HRA list under the Rules, 2026, the house property loss and the full use of deductions have brought the old regime level with the new. If Anita's rent or deductions were slightly lower, the new regime would win. This is a typical case where the computation must be run every year.

Key point

Anita has no business income, so she can choose the regime afresh each year in her return. She should also tell her employer her choice at the start of the year so that TDS is deducted correctly.
