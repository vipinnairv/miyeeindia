6.2 Exempt income (Section 11 and Schedules)

The long list of exemptions in old section 10 is now placed in Schedules. The section simply says that income listed in the Schedules does not form part of total income.

Schedule

Broad content (examples)

Schedule II

Exemptions for individuals and general exemptions, e.g. agricultural income, share of profit from a firm, HRA, gratuity, leave encashment, PPF interest

Schedules III to VII

Exemptions for specified persons and funds, e.g. investment funds, sovereign funds, specified institutions, non-residents in certain cases

Schedule VIII

Political parties and electoral trusts

Illustration 13: Citing an exemption

Under the old law you wrote "exempt under section 10(2A)" for share of profit from a firm. Now you write "exempt under section 11 read with the relevant entry of Schedule II". The exemption itself is unchanged.

Key point

Section 14 (old 14A) still applies: expenses incurred to earn exempt income are not deductible. For companies holding large equity portfolios, compute the disallowance as per the Rules and report it in Form 26.

6.4 Agricultural income and partial integration

Agricultural income is exempt, but for individuals, HUFs, AOPs and BOIs it is added to total income only to decide the rate of tax on non-agricultural income. This is called partial integration. It applies when agricultural income exceeds Rs. 5,000 and non-agricultural income exceeds the basic exemption limit.

Method: (1) Compute tax on non-agricultural income plus agricultural income. (2) Compute tax on agricultural income plus the basic exemption limit. (3) Tax payable is (1) minus (2), then rebate, surcharge and cess are applied.

Judicial view: What is agriculture

CIT v. Raja Benoy Kumar Sahas Roy (Supreme Court, 1957): agriculture requires basic operations on the land itself, such as tilling, sowing and planting, involving human skill and labour. Subsequent operations alone, such as tending trees that grow naturally without any basic operations, do not make the income agricultural.

Illustration 14: Partial integration (old regime, individual below 60)

Suresh has non-agricultural income of Rs. 8,00,000 and agricultural income of Rs. 3,00,000. Compute tax under the old regime.

Step 1: tax on 11,00,000 (8,00,000 plus 3,00,000)

1,42,500

Step 2: tax on 5,50,000 (3,00,000 plus 2,50,000 exemption limit)

22,500

Tax on non-agricultural income (Step 1 less Step 2)

1,20,000

Add: cess at 4%

4,800

Total tax

1,24,800

Without agricultural income, tax on Rs. 8,00,000 under the old regime would have been Rs. 72,500 before cess. The agricultural income, though exempt, pushed the non-agricultural income into higher slabs. Under the new regime the same method applies using the new slabs and the Rs. 4 lakh exemption limit.

Key point

Income from sale of agricultural produce after basic processing (such as drying of tobacco leaves) is agricultural income, but income from a manufacturing process (such as tea manufacture) is partly agricultural. For tea grown and manufactured by the seller, 40% of the income is business income and 60% is agricultural, as per the Rules.

6.5 Expenditure relating to exempt income (section 14)

Section 14 disallows expenditure incurred in relation to exempt income. The Rules prescribe a method where the books do not show a correct figure: broadly, the actual expenditure directly related to exempt income plus 1% of the annual average value of investments that yield exempt income, capped at the total expenditure claimed. The disallowance cannot exceed the exempt income earned in that year.

Judicial view: Maxopp Investment Ltd. v. CIT (Supreme Court, 2018)

The Court held that the disallowance for expenses related to exempt income applies even where shares are held as stock-in-trade, if dividend (then exempt) is earned from them. The dominant purpose of holding the shares does not matter.

Chapter summary

• Income is computed under five heads, then aggregated, losses set off, deductions allowed and tax computed.

• Exemptions are in Schedules II to VII read with section 11.

• Agricultural income is exempt but used for rate purposes through partial integration.

• Expenditure related to exempt income is disallowed under section 14.
