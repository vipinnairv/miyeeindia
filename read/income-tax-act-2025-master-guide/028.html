25.2 The concessional regime under section 200 (old 115BAA)

A domestic company can opt to pay tax at 22% (plus 10% surcharge and 4% cess) if it gives up specified incentives. Once exercised, the option cannot be withdrawn.

  • No deduction for SEZ units (section 144, old 10AA), additional depreciation, tea, coffee and rubber development, scientific research weighted deductions, section 46 (old 35AD), agricultural extension, and profit-linked deductions in sections 138 to 152 (except section 146 for additional employees and, subject to the conditions in the Act, section 148 for inter-corporate dividends).
  • Brought forward losses and depreciation attributable to these disallowed deductions cannot be set off.
  • MAT does not apply. Existing MAT credit can be used only as per the Finance Act 2026 rules explained below.
  • The option is exercised in the prescribed form on or before the return due date of the first year.

25.3 Minimum Alternate Tax and Alternate Minimum Tax (section 206)

MAT (old 115JB) applies to companies that remain in the normal regime. If normal tax on taxable income is lower than the MAT rate on book profit, the company pays MAT. Book profit is the net profit in the statement of profit and loss, adjusted for specified items (income tax provided, deferred tax, reserves, provisions for unascertained liabilities, dividends, exempt incomes, lower of brought forward loss and unabsorbed depreciation, and so on). For Ind AS companies, there are further adjustments for other comprehensive income and transition amounts.

Moving to section 200 saves about Rs. 30 lakh a year. Before 2026 the company might have stayed in the old regime to build MAT credit, but since MAT is now a final tax, that reason has largely gone. If it has old MAT credit, it can use it after switching, up to one fourth of its tax each year.

Practice note

Prepare a multi-year model before advising on the switch: remaining tax holiday years, additional depreciation pipeline, MAT credit balance and its expiry, and expected profits. The section 200 option is irreversible.

MAT is computed and certified in Form 66 (old 29B); AMT in Form 67 (old 29C).

25.4 Tax provisioning in the books

Finance teams must also get the accounting right. Under Ind AS 12 or AS 22, the tax expense has two parts: current tax (the amount payable for the year) and deferred tax (the tax effect of timing differences between book and tax values).

Illustration 98: Deferred tax on depreciation

A company in the section 200 regime (25.168%) has book WDV of plant of Rs. 8 crore and tax WDV of Rs. 6 crore at year-end. Taxable temporary difference = Rs. 2 crore.

Deferred tax liability = 2 crore x 25.168% = Rs. 50,33,600. The movement in this balance during the year is the deferred tax charge or credit in the statement of profit and loss.

Key point

Always prepare an effective tax rate reconciliation (accounting profit x applicable rate, reconciled to actual tax expense). Auditors and investors review it, and it quickly shows missed disallowances or wrong rates.

Surcharge on MAT follows the same thresholds as for normal tax, applied to book profit. MAT is compared with tax on total income under the normal provisions; the higher is payable. From Tax Year 2026-27, no MAT credit arises when MAT is higher.

Judicial view: Book profit

Apollo Tyres Ltd. v. CIT (Supreme Court, 2002): for MAT, the Assessing Officer must accept the profit shown in accounts prepared and certified under the Companies Act, and can make only the adjustments specified in the provision; he cannot recompute the book profit.

Two practical points. First, the Rs. 1,25,000 exemption under section 198 applies to every assessee, including companies. Second, companies in section 200 pay a flat 10% surcharge irrespective of income, which is why the effective rate is 25.168%.

Chapter summary

• Domestic companies choose between normal rates (25% or 30%) and section 200 (22%, effective 25.168%).

• MAT is 14% of book profit from Tax Year 2026-27 and is a final tax.

• Old MAT credit can be used up to one fourth of tax after switching to section 200.

• Model the switch year using multi-year projections; the option is irreversible.
