35.3 TCS highlights (section 394)

  • Most TCS rates rationalised at 2% by the Finance Act 2026, including overseas tour packages (single rate, no threshold).
  • Motor vehicles above Rs. 10 lakh and notified luxury goods: 1%.
  • LRS remittances for education funded by loan and for medical treatment: lower or nil rates.
  • Quarterly TCS statement: Form 143 (old 27EQ).

35.4 General rules that apply to all TDS

  • Time of deduction: for most non-salary payments, at the time of credit to the payee's account (including a suspense or provision account) or at the time of payment, whichever is earlier. For salary, at the time of payment.
  • Amount on which TDS applies: the gross amount, but GST shown separately in the invoice is excluded for most payments (as clarified by the CBDT under the old law).
  • Year-end provisions: a provision for expenses at year-end, where the payee is identifiable and the amount is ascertainable, attracts TDS. Where the payee cannot be identified, the TDS obligation arises when the credit is made to the payee's account.
  • PAN: if the payee does not furnish PAN, or PAN is inoperative (not linked with Aadhaar), TDS is at the higher of 20% and the normal rate (5% for some payments).
  • Lower or nil certificate: where the payee has a certificate under section 395, deduct at the rate stated in it, within its validity and amount.
  • Declarations for no deduction (Form 121): individuals whose tax is nil can give a declaration for interest, dividend and certain other income; seniors can do so even if the income is above the exemption limit, if the tax on estimated total income is nil.

35.5 Contractor payments (old 194C)

"Work" includes advertising, broadcasting and telecasting, carriage of goods or passengers (other than by railways), catering, and manufacturing or supplying a product according to the customer's requirement using material purchased from the customer or its associate. It does not include manufacturing using material purchased from someone other than the customer.

  • Rate: 1% for individuals and HUFs, 2% for others.
  • Threshold: single payment above Rs. 30,000 or aggregate above Rs. 1,00,000 in the year.
  • Where the invoice shows the value of material separately (in a contract where material is supplied by the customer), TDS applies only on the labour or service part.
  • Transporters: no TDS on payments to a contractor in the business of plying, hiring or leasing goods carriages who owns 10 or fewer carriages and furnishes a declaration with PAN.
  • An individual or HUF paying a contractor for personal purposes need not deduct.

Illustration 137: Job work

A garment exporter supplies fabric to a job worker for stitching and pays Rs. 5,00,000 as stitching charges. This is "work" since material is supplied by the customer. TDS at 1% (job worker is an individual) = Rs. 5,000. If the job worker used his own fabric bought from the market and sold finished garments, it would be a purchase of goods, not a contract.

The threshold of Rs. 50,000 applies separately to each category (professional fees, technical fees, royalty, non-compete fees) per payee per year. Director fees have no threshold.

Practice note

Characterisation disputes (contract vs professional vs technical) are among the most common TDS issues. If a payment for a platform or service could be either, document the nature of the service, the contract terms, and why the chosen rate applies. A file note prepared at the time of payment is valuable if the treatment is questioned later.

Judicial view: What are technical services

CIT v. Kotak Securities Ltd. (Supreme Court, 2016): transaction charges paid by a broker to the stock exchange were not fees for technical services, because they were payments for standard facilities available to all members, not services specially rendered to meet the needs of a particular user.

35.7 Rent (old 194-I and 194-IB)

  • Rent includes payment for use of land, building (including factory building), land appurtenant, machinery, plant, equipment, furniture or fittings, whether or not owned by the payee.
  • Rate: 2% for plant, machinery and equipment; 10% for land, building, furniture and fittings.
  • Threshold: rent above Rs. 50,000 for a month or part of a month.
  • Advance rent is subject to TDS when paid; a refundable security deposit is not rent.
  • Individuals and HUFs not liable to tax audit paying rent above Rs. 50,000 a month deduct 2% (old 194-IB), once a year or at the end of tenancy, using Form 141; no TAN needed.

Illustration 138: Rent with GST

A company pays monthly office rent of Rs. 1,00,000 plus GST of Rs. 18,000 (shown separately). TDS at 10% on Rs. 1,00,000 = Rs. 10,000 a month. GST is excluded because it is shown separately.

35.8 Purchase of immovable property (old 194-IA)

  • Buyer deducts 1% on the consideration if it is Rs. 50 lakh or more (for residents). If stamp duty value is higher than consideration, TDS is on the stamp duty value.
  • Where there are multiple buyers or sellers, the Rs. 50 lakh threshold applies to the total consideration for the property, not to each person's share.
  • Consideration includes all charges of the nature of club membership, car parking, electricity and water facility, maintenance fees and advance fees.
  • Challan cum statement in Form 141 within 30 days from the end of the month of deduction; certificate in Form 132 within 15 days thereafter.

Illustration 139: Joint buyers

Husband and wife buy a flat jointly for Rs. 80 lakh, each paying Rs. 40 lakh. Though each share is below Rs. 50 lakh, the total consideration is Rs. 80 lakh, so each deducts 1% on his or her payment: Rs. 40,000 each, and each files Form 141.

35.10 Consequences of TDS default

Default

Consequence

Failure to deduct

Deductor is an "assessee in default" for the tax not deducted; interest 1% a month; disallowance of 30% (residents) or 100% (non-residents) of the expense; penalty up to the tax not deducted

Deducted but not paid

Interest 1.5% a month; recovery with interest; possible prosecution (with safe harbour if paid before the statement due date, as per recent amendments)

Late statement

Fee of Rs. 200 a day; penalty of Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 1,00,000 if delay exceeds one year or statement is incorrect

Payee has paid tax on the income

Deductor is not treated as assessee in default for the tax, if the payee has filed a return including the income and paid tax, supported by an accountant's certificate (old Form 26A); interest still payable up to the date of the payee's return

Judicial view: Recovery when the payee has paid tax

Hindustan Coca Cola Beverage Pvt. Ltd. v. CIT (Supreme Court, 2007): where the payee has already paid tax on the income, the tax cannot be recovered again from the deductor, though interest for the period of default remains payable.

35.11 Quarterly statements, corrections and reconciliation

  • Statements are prepared in the prescribed format and validated through the File Validation Utility before upload.
  • Errors in PAN, challan details or amounts are corrected through correction statements on the TRACES portal.
  • Payees see their TDS in Form 168 (old 26AS) and AIS; mismatches lead to denial of credit in their returns. Deductors should reconcile their books with Form 168 of their own TAN and with the challan status every quarter.
  • Short deduction and late payment notices from TRACES should be reviewed promptly; many arise from wrong PANs or challan tagging and can be cleared by corrections.

Practice note

A quarterly TDS reconciliation (books versus challans versus statements versus Form 168 credits shown to payees) catches most problems before they become demands. For vendors, a Form 168 versus books reconciliation at year-end helps recover missed credits.

Chapter summary

• Section 392 for salary, 393 for other payments with tables and payment codes, 394 for TCS.

• Deduct at the earlier of credit or payment; exclude GST shown separately.

• Defaults bring interest, disallowance, penalty and possible prosecution.

• Reconcile books, challans, statements and Form 168 every quarter.
