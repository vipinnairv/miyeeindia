44. Appeals and Dispute Resolution

44.1 First appeal

Point

Details

Forum

Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) or Joint Commissioner (Appeals) for specified smaller cases; faceless appeal procedure

Appealable orders

Assessment orders, penalty orders, intimations with adjustments, orders treating a person as assessee in default, rectification orders affecting liability

Time limit

30 days from receipt of the order or demand notice; delay can be condoned for sufficient cause

Fee

Rs. 250, Rs. 500 or Rs. 1,000 depending on assessed income

Pre-condition

Tax on returned income must be paid

Additional evidence

Admitted only in specified circumstances (prevented by sufficient cause, not given opportunity), after giving the Assessing Officer a chance to respond

Powers

Confirm, reduce, enhance (after notice) or annul the assessment

44.2 Stay of demand

Filing an appeal does not automatically stay recovery. The taxpayer applies to the Assessing Officer for stay. As per CBDT instructions, stay is generally granted on payment of 20% of the disputed demand, with the possibility of a lower amount in deserving cases (for example, where the addition is covered by a decision of a higher court in the taxpayer's favour). The Tribunal can also grant stay for up to 365 days.

44.3 Income Tax Appellate Tribunal

  • Appeal against the order of the Commissioner (Appeals) or DRP directions, within 2 months from the end of the month in which the order is received.
  • Fee of Rs. 500 to Rs. 10,000 depending on assessed income, and Rs. 500 for stay applications.
  • The other party can file cross objections within 30 days of receiving notice of the appeal.
  • The Tribunal is the final fact-finding authority. Its decisions on law are binding on lower authorities in its jurisdiction until reversed.

44.4 High Court and Supreme Court

An appeal to the High Court lies only on a substantial question of law, within 120 days. An appeal to the Supreme Court lies against a High Court judgment on a certificate of fitness or by special leave. The department does not file appeals where the tax effect is below monetary limits set by the CBDT (recently Rs. 60 lakh for the Tribunal, Rs. 2 crore for High Courts and Rs. 5 crore for the Supreme Court, with exceptions).

44.5 Alternative dispute resolution

Mechanism

Who can use it

Key features

Dispute Resolution Panel

Eligible assessees: TP cases, foreign companies

Objections to draft order; binding directions within 9 months

Dispute Resolution Committee

Small taxpayers: returned income up to Rs. 50 lakh and disputed variation up to Rs. 10 lakh (excluding search and certain cases)

Faceless resolution; can reduce or waive penalty

Mutual Agreement Procedure

Residents of treaty countries facing double taxation

Negotiation between competent authorities; can run with suspended collection subject to bank guarantee

Advance rulings

Non-residents and specified residents on transactions

Binding ruling on the transaction, through the Board for Advance Rulings

Advance pricing agreement

Taxpayers with international transactions

Prospective certainty with rollback

Practice note

Before appealing, compare the cost of litigation (fees, time, deposit of 20%) with the tax at stake and the strength of the legal position. For recurring issues, a well-drafted appeal in the first year with the right evidence often sets the tone for later years.

Judicial view: Appeals: powers, delay and stay

CIT v. Kapurchand Shrimal (Supreme Court, 1981): the powers of the first appellate authority are co-terminous with those of the Assessing Officer; it can do what the officer could have done and can direct him to do what he failed to do.

Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji (Supreme Court, 1987): a liberal and justice-oriented approach should be taken in condoning delay; a meritorious matter should not be thrown out at the threshold for technical reasons.

PCIT v. LG Electronics India (P) Ltd. (Supreme Court, 2018): the administrative instruction that stay may be granted on payment of 20% of the demand is not a rigid rule; the authorities can grant stay on a lower payment in appropriate cases.

CIT v. Chhabil Dass Agarwal (Supreme Court, 2013): where an effective statutory appeal is available, a writ petition is generally not entertained, except in exceptional situations such as violation of natural justice or lack of jurisdiction.

Chapter summary

• First appeal within 30 days; Tribunal within 2 months from end of month; High Court only on substantial questions of law.

• Stay usually on 20% payment.

• DRP, DRC, MAP, advance rulings and APAs are alternatives to litigation.
