7. Salaries (Sections 15 to 19)

7.1 What is salary

Salary includes wages, pension, annuity, gratuity, fees, commission, perquisites, profits in lieu of salary, advance salary and leave encashment. Section 16 defines salary, section 17 covers perquisites and section 18 covers profits in lieu of salary. Section 19 lists the deductions and exemptions available from salary. Salary is taxable on due or receipt basis, whichever is earlier.

Key point

Employer contribution to NPS (section 124, old 80CCD(2)) is allowed in the new regime too, up to 14% of salary.

7.4 Perquisites in brief

  • Rent-free accommodation, car, free meals above limits, ESOPs, interest-free loans and club facilities are valued under the Income-tax Rules, 2026. The employer reports them in Form 123 (old 12BA).
  • ESOP: taxable as perquisite when shares are allotted (FMV on exercise date less amount paid). Later sale gives capital gains with cost equal to that FMV.
  • A vehicle provided for travel between home and office is excluded from perquisites under the 2025 Act, clarifying the earlier position.
  • Employees declare investments and HRA details to the employer in Form 124 (old 12BB).

Illustration 16: ESOP

Arjun exercises 1,000 options at Rs. 100 each on 1 August 2026 when the FMV is Rs. 600. Perquisite = 1,000 x (600 less 100) = Rs. 5,00,000, taxed as salary with TDS under section 392.

He sells the shares in September 2027 at Rs. 900. Capital gain = 1,000 x (900 less 600) = Rs. 3,00,000. Holding period counts from allotment.

7.5 Relief for arrears (section 157, old 89)

When salary arrears are received in a later year, tax can go up because of higher slabs. Relief is available by recomputing tax as if the arrears were taxed in the years they relate to. The employee files the relief form (old Form 10E, now Form 39) before filing the return.

7.6 Is it salary at all? Employer and employee relationship

Income is taxed as salary only if there is an employer and employee relationship. Payments to consultants, retainers and independent professionals are business or professional income, taxed under a different head and subject to TDS under section 393 rather than 392. The courts look at the degree of control over how the work is done, integration into the organisation, the right to terminate, and whether the person works for others as well.

Illustration 17: Director remuneration

A whole time director of a company receives a monthly salary under an employment contract. This is salary. An independent director receives sitting fees and commission for attending board meetings; he is not an employee, so this is income from other sources or profession, with TDS at 10% under section 393 (old 194J).

Judicial view: Director as employee

Ram Prashad v. CIT (Supreme Court, 1972): whether a managing director is an employee depends on the terms of his appointment. Where he works under the control and supervision of the board, the relationship is one of employment and the remuneration is salary; where he acts as an agent with independent powers, it may not be.

7.7 Allowances under the Income-tax Rules, 2026

Allowances are fixed cash amounts paid by the employer. They are fully taxable unless a specific exemption applies. Judicial view: Valuation of perquisites

Arun Kumar v. Union of India (Supreme Court, 2006): upheld the Rules for valuing rent-free accommodation. A perquisite is a benefit or advantage given by the employer in connection with employment, and the Rules supply the machinery to value it; the valuation in the Rules is binding.

Illustration 19: Company car and loan

Kavya, a manager, is provided a 2.0 litre car with driver for official and personal use; all expenses are paid by the employer. She also has a housing loan from the employer of Rs. 10,00,000 at 4% when the SBI rate is 9%.

Car perquisite = (7,000 plus 3,000) x 12 = Rs. 1,20,000.

Loan perquisite = 10,00,000 x (9% less 4%) = Rs. 50,000 (the Rs. 2 lakh exemption does not apply since the loan exceeds it).

Total perquisites added to salary = Rs. 1,70,000. These values are taxable in both regimes.

Illustration 20: Employer retirement contributions above Rs. 7.5 lakh

A senior executive's employer contributes Rs. 4,50,000 to PF, Rs. 3,00,000 to NPS and Rs. 1,50,000 to a superannuation fund, total Rs. 9,00,000. The excess of Rs. 1,50,000 over Rs. 7,50,000 is a taxable perquisite, and the annual accretion (interest or returns) on that excess is also taxable each year, computed as per the Rules.

Illustration 21: Gratuity

Ramesh retires from a private company covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act after 24 years and 8 months of service. Last drawn basic plus DA = Rs. 1,04,000 a month. Gratuity received = Rs. 16,00,000.

Years of service = 25 (8 months exceeds 6 months). Formula amount = 15/26 x 1,04,000 x 25 = Rs. 15,00,000.

Exempt = least of 16,00,000, 15,00,000 and 20,00,000 = Rs. 15,00,000. Taxable = Rs. 1,00,000.

Leave encashment on retirement

For non-government employees, the exemption is the least of: actual amount; 10 months' average salary; cash equivalent of unavailed leave calculated at 30 days for every year of service; and Rs. 25,00,000 (lifetime limit). Leave encashment while in service is fully taxable.

Illustration 22: Leave encashment

Ramesh also receives Rs. 12,00,000 for 300 days of accumulated leave. His entitlement was 30 days a year and he availed 450 days in 25 years. Average salary for the last 10 months = Rs. 1,00,000.

Leave to credit on the 30 days basis = (25 x 30) less 450 = 300 days = 10 months. Cash equivalent = 10 x 1,00,000 = Rs. 10,00,000. Ten months' average salary = Rs. 10,00,000.

Exempt = least of 12,00,000; 10,00,000; 10,00,000; 25,00,000 = Rs. 10,00,000. Taxable = Rs. 2,00,000.

Pension

Uncommuted (monthly) pension is fully taxable as salary. Commuted pension (lump sum) is fully exempt for government employees. For others, one third of the full commuted value is exempt if the employee also receives gratuity, and one half if he does not.

Illustration 23: Commuted pension

Ramesh commutes 40% of his pension and receives Rs. 8,00,000. The full commuted value (100%) is therefore Rs. 20,00,000. Since he also received gratuity, exempt amount = one third of 20,00,000 = Rs. 6,66,667. Taxable = Rs. 1,33,333.

VRS and retrenchment compensation

Compensation on voluntary retirement under a scheme that meets the prescribed conditions is exempt up to Rs. 5,00,000, once in a lifetime. Retrenchment compensation is exempt up to the least of the amount under the Industrial Disputes Act formula, Rs. 5,00,000 and the actual amount.

Practice note

When an employee withdraws EPF before completing 5 years, TDS applies at 10% above Rs. 50,000 if PAN is given. Check whether the service with earlier employers was transferred; transferred service counts towards the 5 years.

7.11 Relief for arrears of salary (section 157) with a worked example

When salary relating to earlier years is received in a later year, the whole amount is taxed in the year of receipt, possibly at a higher slab. Relief is computed as follows:

  • (1) Tax on total income of the current year including arrears, less tax on total income of the current year excluding arrears.
  • (2) Tax on total income of the earlier year including the arrears relating to it, less tax on total income of that year excluding them.
  • Relief = (1) less (2), if positive. The employee must file Form 39 (old 10E) before filing the return.

Illustration 24: Arrears relief

Leela receives arrears of Rs. 3,00,000 in Tax Year 2026-27 relating to FY 2024-25 due to a pay revision. Her taxable income for Tax Year 2026-27 without arrears is Rs. 11,00,000 (new regime), and for FY 2024-25 it was Rs. 7,00,000 (new regime as then applicable).

Tax Year 2026-27: tax on Rs. 14,00,000 = Rs. 90,000 before cess (no rebate, as income exceeds Rs. 12 lakh). Tax on Rs. 11,00,000 = nil (rebate). Difference = Rs. 90,000 plus cess.

FY 2024-25: compute tax on Rs. 10,00,000 and Rs. 7,00,000 using that year's slabs and rebate (rebate then available up to Rs. 7 lakh). Under those slabs, tax on Rs. 10,00,000 was Rs. 50,000 and on Rs. 7,00,000 nil after rebate. Difference = Rs. 50,000 plus cess.

Relief = Rs. 90,000 less Rs. 50,000 = Rs. 40,000 plus the related cess. Filing Form 39 is essential; without it the CPC will not allow the relief.

7.13 ESOPs and RSUs of a foreign parent

Many employees of Indian subsidiaries of multinationals receive stock options or restricted stock units (RSUs) of the foreign parent. The taxation follows a three-stage pattern:

Stage

Tax effect in India

Grant

No tax

Vesting and allotment (RSU) or exercise and allotment (option)

Perquisite = FMV on the date of exercise or allotment less amount paid. For shares listed abroad, FMV is based on the price on the foreign exchange. TDS under section 392 by the Indian employer

Sale

Capital gain: sale value less FMV taken as perquisite. Foreign shares are long-term after 24 months; LTCG at 12.5%; STCG at slab rates

Dividends

Taxable at slab rates; foreign tax withheld (for example, US tax of 25% withheld on dividends to Indian residents under the treaty) is claimed as foreign tax credit

Reporting

Shares and dividends must be reported in Schedule FA (calendar year basis) and Schedule FSI; missing this can attract the Black Money Act penalty

Illustration 25: RSUs of a US parent

Arun receives 200 RSUs of a US listed parent that vest on 15 June 2026 when the share price is USD 150 and the rupee rate is 84. Perquisite = 200 x 150 x 84 = Rs. 25,20,000, added to salary and subject to TDS. The US broker may sell some shares to cover US withholding only if required; generally no US tax applies on vesting for an Indian resident.

He sells the 200 shares on 20 August 2028 at USD 180 (rate 86). Sale value = Rs. 30,96,000. Cost = Rs. 25,20,000. Holding is more than 24 months, so LTCG = Rs. 5,76,000, taxed at 12.5%.

Chapter summary

• Salary requires an employer and employee relationship; consultants are taxed under business or profession.

• Standard deduction: Rs. 75,000 (new) and Rs. 50,000 (old).

• Rules, 2026 raised allowance limits and added Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune to the 50% HRA cities.

• Perquisites are valued under the Rules; car, loan, meal and gift values were revised.

• Retirement benefits have specific exemption formulas; employer contributions above Rs. 7.5 lakh are taxable.

• Arrears relief under section 157 requires Form 39 before filing the return.
