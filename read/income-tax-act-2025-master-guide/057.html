54. Case Study 7: Merger of Group Companies

Facts. Aarav Industries Ltd. (a manufacturer) proposes to amalgamate its wholly owned subsidiary Aarav Components Pvt. Ltd. (also a manufacturer) into itself with the appointed date 1 April 2026. Aarav Components has: business loss of Tax Year 2022-23 of Rs. 6 crore; unabsorbed depreciation of Rs. 2 crore; plant WDV of Rs. 15 crore; land (book value Rs. 3 crore, market value Rs. 12 crore). It has been in business for 7 years and held all its fixed assets throughout.

54.1 Tax analysis

Issue

Analysis

Is it an amalgamation?

Yes: all assets and liabilities pass; the parent holds 100%, so the 75% shareholder test is met by excluding shares held by the amalgamated company

Capital gains on transfer of land and plant

Not a transfer under section 70; no tax on the Rs. 9 crore embedded gain in land

Cost and holding period for the parent

Land at Rs. 3 crore with the subsidiary's holding period; plant block at WDV Rs. 15 crore

Losses and depreciation (section 116)

Industrial undertaking, in business 3 or more years, held 75% of fixed assets for 2 years: conditions met. Parent must hold 75% of the assets and continue the business for 5 years

Remaining life of the loss

Loss of Tax Year 2022-23 can be set off only up to Tax Year 2030-31. Unabsorbed depreciation has no time limit

Expenses of the merger

Legal and filing costs deductible over 5 years under section 52

Stamp duty

Payable on the Tribunal order as per state law; not a tax deduction until incurred as expense

Procedure

Notice of the scheme to the department; returns for Tax Year 2026-27 filed by the parent including the subsidiary's income; Form 29 for loss carry forward

Value. The loss and depreciation of Rs. 8 crore, used against the parent's profits in the normal regime (29.12% for the parent's turnover level), are worth about Rs. 2.33 crore of tax, provided the conditions are maintained for 5 years.
