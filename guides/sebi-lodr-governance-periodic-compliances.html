SEBI (LODR) Corporate Governance and Periodic Compliances: Regulations 13(3), 23(9), 27(2), 31 and 33

Authored by VipinPublished 15 Oct 2026

A working manual for listed entities on the main board: governance architecture, the quarterly and half-yearly filing calendar, related party controls, event disclosures under Regulation 30 and the SEBI standard operating procedure for fines.

Who this is for: company secretaries, CFOs, compliance officers, independent directors and audit committee members of companies whose specified securities are listed on the main board of a recognised stock exchange. SME-listed entities have important relaxations, which are noted where relevant.

1. Statutory Overview and Legislative Intent

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015 (LODR) were framed under Sections 11, 11A and 30 of the SEBI Act, 1992 and Section 31 of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956. They replaced the old equity listing agreement with enforceable regulations. Their intent is simple: once a company takes money from the public, it must keep the public informed continuously, treat all shareholders equally and be supervised by a board that is genuinely independent of management and promoters.

The governance architecture in brief

  • Regulation 17: board composition with an optimum combination of executive and non-executive directors, at least one woman director (an independent woman director for the top 1000 listed entities by market capitalisation), at least half independent directors where the chairperson is executive or a promoter (one-third otherwise), a minimum of six directors for the top 2000 entities, at least four board meetings a year with a gap of not more than 120 days, and a quorum of one-third or three directors, whichever is higher.
  • Regulation 17A: a person cannot be a director in more than seven listed entities, or an independent director in more than seven (three if he or she is a whole-time director or managing director of a listed entity).
  • Regulations 18 to 21: audit committee, nomination and remuneration committee, stakeholders relationship committee and risk management committee (top 1000 entities).
  • Regulation 23: related party transactions policy, approvals and disclosures.
  • Regulation 24A: secretarial audit and annual secretarial compliance report.
  • Regulation 25: obligations of independent directors, including separate meetings and directors and officers insurance for the top 1000 entities.
  • Regulation 30: disclosure of material events and information.

Related party definitions widened from 1 April 2023. Every person or entity forming part of the promoter or promoter group is a related party irrespective of shareholding, and so is any person holding 10% or more in the listed entity, directly or on a beneficial interest basis, at any time during the immediately preceding financial year. SEBI has also approved changes to the materiality thresholds that scale with turnover; confirm the text of Regulation 23(1) in force for your reporting period on sebi.gov.in before applying the Rs. 1,000 crore test.

3. Step-by-Step Compliance and Filing Workflow

A. The quarterly results cycle (Regulations 29, 30, 33 and 47)

  1. Trading window closure. Close the trading window for designated persons from the end of the quarter under the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015. It reopens 48 hours after the results are made public.
  2. Prior intimation (Regulation 29). Inform the stock exchanges at least two working days in advance, excluding the date of intimation and the date of the meeting, of the board meeting at which financial results will be considered.
  3. Limited review or audit. The statutory auditor performs a limited review of quarterly results (or audit, where the entity opts). Consolidated results must also be reviewed. At least 80% of each consolidated line item must be subject to audit or limited review.
  4. Audit committee and board approval. The audit committee reviews results before they go to the board. The CEO and CFO certify the results do not contain false or misleading statements (Regulation 33(2)).
  5. Outcome within 30 minutes. Submit the board meeting outcome and results to the exchanges within 30 minutes of the conclusion of the meeting under Regulation 30. Results are filed in PDF and in XBRL on the NSE NEAPS portal and the BSE Listing Centre.
  6. Publication. Publish results as required by Regulation 47 and upload them on the company website under Regulation 46.

B. Shareholding pattern (Regulation 31)

  1. Obtain the beneficial position (BENPOS) as on the last day of the quarter from the RTA.
  2. Classify holdings into promoter and promoter group, public (institutions, non-institutions) and non-promoter non-public (employee trusts, custodians), with details of pledges and encumbrances and shares held in demat form. Promoter and promoter group holdings must be entirely in demat form.
  3. File in the prescribed XBRL format within 21 days of the end of each quarter, and also one day before listing on any exchange and within 10 days of any capital restructuring that changes total issued shares by more than 2%.

C. Investor grievances (Regulation 13)

  1. Register on SCORES and ensure that every complaint is redressed within the timelines of the SEBI SCORES framework.
  2. Compile the quarterly statement: pending at the beginning of the quarter, received, disposed of and remaining unresolved at the end of the quarter.
  3. File with the exchanges within 21 days of the end of each quarter (Regulation 13(3)), and place it before the board (Regulation 13(4)).

D. Corporate governance compliance report (Regulation 27(2))

  1. Prepare the report in the format specified by SEBI covering board composition, committees, meetings, related party transactions, affirmations and any non-compliance with reasons.
  2. Sign by the compliance officer or the CEO and file within 21 days of the end of each quarter.

E. Related party transactions (Regulation 23)

  1. Adopt a board-approved RPT policy, including clear materiality thresholds and the definition of material modifications. Review it at least once every three years.
  2. Obtain prior approval of the audit committee for all RPTs and subsequent material modifications. Only independent directors on the audit committee may approve. Omnibus approvals are permitted for repetitive transactions, valid for one year, subject to the criteria in the policy and the SEBI master circular.
  3. For material RPTs and material modifications, obtain prior shareholder approval by resolution; related parties must not vote to approve, regardless of whether they are party to the particular transaction.
  4. Disclose RPTs to the exchanges every half year under Regulation 23(9) in the format specified by SEBI, on the date of publication of financial results for the half year, and publish on the website.
Integrated filings: SEBI introduced an integrated filing system in which governance-related quarterly filings (including the corporate governance report and investor grievance statement) and financial filings are submitted through consolidated formats on the exchange portals. The timelines in this article remain the anchor; always check the current exchange circular for the combined due date and the format to use.

5. Interest, Late Fees and Penal Consequences

LODR enforcement works on three levels: exchange fines under the SEBI standard operating procedure (SOP), SEBI adjudication under the SEBI Act, and in serious cases directions under Sections 11 and 11B.

Persistent non-compliance escalates: after continued default the exchanges may freeze the promoter and promoter group holdings, move the scrip to a restricted trading segment, and ultimately suspend trading. Suspension for more than six months can lead to compulsory delisting. SEBI periodically revises these amounts; always confirm against the current master circular.

6. Practical Audit Pitfalls and Best Practices

  • Keep the board composition tested monthly. A single resignation of an independent director can breach Regulation 17 overnight. Regulation 25(6) requires the vacancy to be filled within three months, and the fine meter runs from the date of breach.
  • Build the related party master from multiple sources: MBP-1 disclosures, Regulation 7 PIT disclosures, promoter group declarations, shareholding of 10% or more, subsidiaries' related parties, and Ind AS 24. Update it every quarter and share it with accounts payable so that transactions are flagged at invoice entry.
  • Track cumulative RPT values against omnibus limits. Overshooting an omnibus approval mid-year is a breach. Use a monthly report to the audit committee.
  • Reconcile results with XBRL. Exchanges compare PDF and XBRL filings. Mismatches lead to clarification requests and, in some cases, fines for incomplete filings.
  • Prepare for Regulation 30 by event type. Maintain a board-approved materiality policy and an event log. Define who decides materiality out of hours, because the 12 and 24 hour clocks do not stop for weekends.
  • Respond to media rumours with care. The top 250 listed entities must confirm, deny or clarify reported events within 24 hours of a material price movement, under Regulation 30(11).
  • Treat the annual secretarial compliance report as a health check. Fix the observations in the report before the next one. Repeated observations attract regulatory attention.
  • Archive everything. Regulation 9 requires a document preservation policy. Exchanges and SEBI can ask for records many years later during investigations.
