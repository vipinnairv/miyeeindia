TDS and TCS Master Matrix with Return Schedules: Sections 192 to 195, 206C, Challan Due Dates and Forms 24Q, 26Q, 27Q and 27EQ

Authored by VipinPublished 25 Aug 2026

A practitioner's matrix of TDS and TCS sections, thresholds and rates as rationalised from 1 April 2025 and carried into the consolidated provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025, with deposit dates, quarterly returns, certificates, interest, fees and disallowances.

Read this first: the law changed on 1 April 2026. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 with effect from 1 April 2026. FY 2026-27 is therefore the first tax year under the new Act; the separate concepts of "previous year" and "assessment year" have been replaced by a single "tax year", so what practitioners would have called AY 2027-28 is now simply the tax year 2026-27, with returns filed in 2027. The new Act largely carries forward the substance of the old provisions but renumbers and regroups them. In this guide, section numbers of the 1961 Act are used as familiar reference labels (for example "Section 44AD") because most forms, case law, software and professionals still use them; the corresponding provision in the 2025 Act should be confirmed using the official section mapping published on incometax.gov.in. Returns for FY 2025-26 filed during 2026 remain governed by the 1961 Act.

1. Statutory Overview and Legislative Intent

Tax deduction at source (TDS) and tax collection at source (TCS) make the payer or seller an agent of the Government. Tax is collected at the point where income arises, which brings payees into the tax net, spreads collections across the year and creates a data trail that now feeds the Annual Information Statement. Chapter XVII-B (deduction) and Chapter XVII-BB (collection) of the 1961 Act contained over thirty separate sections. The Income-tax Act, 2025 consolidates them into tabular provisions (Section 393 for deduction and Section 394 for collection, with related provisions for certificates, statements and consequences), but the payment categories, thresholds and rates largely continue from the position after the Finance Act, 2025.

The Finance Act, 2025 rationalised many thresholds with effect from 1 April 2025, removed the higher-rate provisions for non-filers (Sections 206AB and 206CCA), and omitted TCS on sale of goods under Section 206C(1H). These changes are reflected below.

3. Step-by-Step Compliance and Filing Workflow

  1. Obtain a TAN by applying in Form 49B through the Protean (formerly NSDL) or UTIITSL portal. Every deductor and collector must quote TAN in challans, returns and certificates.
  2. Register the TAN on the e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and on TRACES (tdscpc.gov.in).
  3. Configure vendor and payee masters with PAN, status (individual, company, firm), residential status, section mapping and lower deduction certificate details (Section 197 certificates are verified on TRACES).
  4. Deduct at the earlier of credit or payment for most sections (Sections 194A, 194C, 194H, 194-I, 194J); at the time of payment for Sections 192, 194-IA, 194-IB and 194M; and on credit or payment of amounts exceeding the threshold for Section 194Q. Credit to a suspense or provision account counts as credit.
  5. Deposit through the e-Pay Tax service on the e-filing portal using Challan ITNS 281 (minor head 200 for TDS payable by taxpayer, 400 for TDS on regular assessment). Use Form 26QB, 26QC, 26QD or 26QE challan-cum-statements for Sections 194-IA, 194-IB, 194M and 194S respectively.
  6. Prepare quarterly statements using the Return Preparation Utility and validate with the File Validation Utility (FVU). Form 24Q for salary, 26Q for other resident payments, 27Q for non-residents and 27EQ for TCS. Salary details for the full year are reported in Annexure II of the fourth quarter Form 24Q.
  7. Upload the validated file on the e-filing portal with DSC or EVC.
  8. Process defaults: download the justification report from TRACES, correct short deduction, short payment, interest and PAN errors through correction statements, and pay outstanding demands.
  9. Issue certificates: Form 16 (salary, annual), Form 16A (non-salary, quarterly), Form 16B, 16C, 16D and 16E for the challan-cum-statement sections, and Form 27D for TCS. Certificates must be downloaded from TRACES.
  10. For payments to non-residents, file Form 15CA online before remittance, with Form 15CB from a Chartered Accountant where the remittance is chargeable to tax and exceeds Rs. 5 lakh (unless exempted under Rule 37BB).

Worked example: classification, year-end provision and interest

A company engages an IT vendor for software maintenance at Rs. 40,000 a month and a lawyer for a one-time opinion at Rs. 60,000 in March.

  • IT maintenance: routine maintenance and support is generally treated as fees for technical services, attracting 2% under Section 194J once the annual payments in that category exceed Rs. 50,000. The first two months (Rs. 80,000 cumulative) cross the threshold in the second month, so TDS applies on the full amount credited from that point onwards, including the first month's credit, which should be covered when deduction is made on the second credit.
  • Legal opinion: fees for professional services attract 10% under Section 194J as the Rs. 60,000 exceeds the Rs. 50,000 threshold. If the invoice is received in April but the expense is provided for in the March books, TDS is due on the provision credited on 31 March, and the deposit date is 30 April.
  • Interest on delay: if the company deducts Rs. 6,000 on 31 March but deposits it on 15 June, interest under Section 201(1A)(ii) runs at 1.5% per month from the date of deduction to the date of payment, counting a part of a month as a full month: March (part), April, May and June (part), that is 4 months. Interest is Rs. 6,000 x 1.5% x 4 = Rs. 360, payable before the statement is filed. The Q4 statement is due on 31 May; if it is filed on 15 June, the fee under Section 234E is Rs. 200 x 15 days = Rs. 3,000.
  • Disallowance: because the tax was deposited before the due date of the return, the expense is not disallowed under Section 40(a)(ia).

6. Practical Audit Pitfalls and Best Practices

  • Distinguish technical from professional services correctly. The 2% versus 10% split under Section 194J depends on the nature of the service. Document the basis for classification in the vendor master; short deduction attracts interest and disallowance.
  • Avoid double deduction under Sections 194Q and 194-O. Where 194-O applies, 194Q does not. Where the seller collects TCS on goods and the buyer is also liable under 194Q, the buyer's TDS takes precedence (relevant for earlier years when 206C(1H) existed).
  • Year-end provisions: TDS applies on provisions credited to payee accounts or expense provisions where the payee is identifiable. Deduct and deposit by 30 April to avoid disallowance.
  • Reconcile Form 26AS and AIS of the company as a deductee. Customers' TDS must appear in the company's Form 26AS before credit is claimed in the return.
  • Match the books with TRACES every quarter. Pay small interest defaults immediately rather than letting them accumulate into demands with additional interest.
  • Keep Form 10F, TRC and no-PE declarations current for treaty claims, renewed each year, and conduct a principal purpose test review under the Multilateral Instrument where relevant.
  • Lower deduction certificates under Section 197 must be validated on TRACES for the period and amount. Deduct at normal rates once the certificate amount is exhausted.
  • Plan the transition. From tax year 2026-27, form and section numbers in software change under the 2025 Act and its Rules. Test the updated utilities before the first quarterly statement is due on 31 July 2026.

  • Finance Act, 2025 (threshold rationalisation from 1 April 2025, omission of Sections 206AB, 206CCA and 206C(1H), changes to Section 276B).

  • Income-tax Rules, 1962: Rules 30, 31, 31A, 37BB and 37CA, and Forms 16, 16A, 24Q, 26Q, 27Q, 27EQ, 15CA, 15CB and 49B (and corresponding Rules and forms under the 2025 Act).
