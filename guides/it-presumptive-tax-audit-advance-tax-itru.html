Presumptive Taxation, Tax Audit, Advance Tax and Updated Returns: Sections 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE, 44AB, 43B(h), 234A, 234B, 234C and ITR-U

Authored by VipinPublished 1 Sept 2026

The small business and professional's compliance core: who can use presumptive schemes and the 5% cash test, when a tax audit is compulsory, the MSME payment disallowance, advance tax instalments and interest, and the four-year updated return window.

Read this first: the law changed on 1 April 2026. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 with effect from 1 April 2026. FY 2026-27 is therefore the first tax year under the new Act; the separate concepts of "previous year" and "assessment year" have been replaced by a single "tax year", so what practitioners would have called AY 2027-28 is now simply the tax year 2026-27, with returns filed in 2027. The new Act largely carries forward the substance of the old provisions but renumbers and regroups them. In this guide, section numbers of the 1961 Act are used as familiar reference labels (for example "Section 44AD") because most forms, case law, software and professionals still use them; the corresponding provision in the 2025 Act should be confirmed using the official section mapping published on incometax.gov.in. Returns for FY 2025-26 filed during 2026 remain governed by the 1961 Act.

1. Statutory Overview and Legislative Intent

Small businesses and professionals form the majority of taxpayers with business income, yet many cannot afford full bookkeeping and audit. Presumptive taxation offers a deal: declare income at a fixed percentage of turnover, and the law dispenses with books of account and audit. Larger businesses get the opposite deal: a tax audit by a Chartered Accountant, reported in a detailed form that gives the department structured data. Advance tax ensures that tax is paid as income is earned, and the updated return gives taxpayers a paid route to correct omissions without litigation.

  • Section 44AD: presumptive income for eligible businesses at 8% of turnover (6% for digital receipts).
  • Section 44ADA: presumptive income for specified professionals at 50% of gross receipts.
  • Section 44AE: presumptive income for owners of goods carriages.
  • Section 44AA: maintenance of books of account.
  • Section 44AB: compulsory tax audit.
  • Section 43B(h): deduction for payments to micro and small enterprises only on actual payment when made beyond the time allowed by Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006.
  • Sections 207 to 211: advance tax liability and instalments; Sections 234A, 234B and 234C: interest.
  • Section 139(8A) and Section 140B: updated return and additional tax.

3. Step-by-Step Compliance and Filing Workflow

A. Using a presumptive scheme

  1. Confirm eligibility: entity type, nature of activity and turnover. Compute the share of cash receipts to test the higher limits.
  2. Track receipts by mode. Digital receipts received by the due date of return qualify for the 6% rate under Section 44AD.
  3. Even without books, keep bank statements, sales invoices, GST returns and a simple record of cash receipts. GST turnover and AIS data are matched with the turnover declared.
  4. Pay advance tax in full by 15 March.
  5. File ITR-4 (Sugam) where eligible (resident individual, HUF or firm other than LLP with total income up to Rs. 50 lakh), or ITR-3 or ITR-5 otherwise. Report the turnover, presumptive income, and balance sheet items (sundry debtors, creditors, stock and cash) in the presumptive schedule.

B. Tax audit

  1. The assessee maintains books under Section 44AA and Rule 6F (for professionals), and appoints a Chartered Accountant for the tax audit. ICAI limits a CA to 60 tax audit assignments in a year.
  2. The auditor prepares Form 3CA (where accounts are already audited under another law, such as the Companies Act) or Form 3CB (other cases), along with Form 3CD, a statement of particulars covering more than 40 clauses: nature of business, method of accounting, changes in stock valuation, depreciation, Section 40A(3) cash payments, Section 43B items including MSME payments under 43B(h) (clause 26) and MSME interest disclosure (clause 22), TDS compliance, loans and deposits under Sections 269SS and 269T, and quantitative details.
  3. The auditor uploads the report on the e-filing portal with UDIN and DSC; the assessee accepts it from his login.
  4. The return is filed after the audit report, by the due date.

C. Advance tax

  1. Estimate total income for the year, including capital gains and other income, and compute tax after TDS and TCS.
  2. If the remaining liability is Rs. 10,000 or more, pay advance tax through Challan 280 on the e-Pay Tax service, selecting minor head 100 (advance tax). Resident senior citizens without business or professional income are exempt (Section 207(2)).
  3. Revise the estimate each quarter and pay instalments as per the schedule. Capital gains or dividend arising after an instalment date are included in subsequent instalments, without interest for the earlier shortfall attributable to them.

D. Filing an updated return

  1. Check eligibility. An updated return cannot be filed where it is a return of loss, reduces the tax liability, increases a refund, results in or increases a loss, where search or survey has been initiated, where assessment or reassessment is pending or completed for that year, or where the department has information under specified laws. Where a notice under Section 148A has been issued and an order has been passed holding that it is not a fit case for reassessment, an updated return may be filed (as clarified by the Finance Act, 2025).
  2. Compute tax and interest on the updated income, then compute additional tax under Section 140B.
  3. Pay tax, interest, fee and additional tax through Challan 280 under self-assessment.
  4. File the applicable ITR form with Form ITR-U on the e-filing portal, with the reason for updating (income not reported, wrong head, wrong rate, reduction of carried-forward loss, and so on). Only one updated return may be filed for an assessment year.

6. Practical Audit Pitfalls and Best Practices

  • Turnover consistency. GST turnover, bank credits and the turnover in the return must reconcile. Differences explained by exempt supplies, advances or reimbursements should be documented.
  • Test the 5% cash condition early. The higher limits for Sections 44AD, 44ADA and 44AB depend on the share of cash. A few large cash receipts near year end can push a business into audit.
  • Do not combine Sections 44AD and 44ADA for the same activity, and remember that LLPs and companies cannot use either.
  • Build a vendor MSME register. Collect Udyam certificates, tag micro and small vendors, track payment terms and ensure payment within 15 or 45 days, especially around 31 March, to avoid Section 43B(h) disallowance.
  • Keep advance tax workings. A short memo with the estimate at each instalment date helps defend interest computations and supports any claim of income arising after the instalment.
  • Use the updated return proactively. If AIS shows income that was missed, an updated return within 12 months costs 25% additional tax, which is usually far less than penalty and litigation.
  • Plan for the new Act. Clauses of Form 3CD and the presumptive schedules in the ITR are being aligned with the Income-tax Act, 2025. Review the updated forms before planning the audit timetable for tax year 2026-27.
