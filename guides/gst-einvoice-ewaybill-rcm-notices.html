e-Invoicing, e-Way Bills, Reverse Charge and Departmental Notices: IRN Rules, the 30-Day IRP Limit, e-Way Bill Validity, Notified RCM Services, ASMT-10 and DRC-01

Authored by VipinPublished 2 Oct 2026

The operational controls that decide whether invoices are valid and goods can move, the reverse charge obligations businesses most often miss, and a practical method for answering scrutiny and demand notices.

Three systems, one data trail: the e-invoice (IRP), the e-way bill system and the GST return system now talk to each other. Department analytics compare all three automatically. Errors in one surface as notices in another.

1. Statutory Overview and Legislative Intent

e-Invoicing

Rule 48(4) of the CGST Rules requires notified classes of registered persons to prepare invoices by uploading specified particulars to the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) and obtaining an Invoice Reference Number (IRN). Under Rule 48(5), an invoice issued without following this procedure is not a valid invoice. The intent is to capture B2B transactions at source, reduce fake invoicing, and pre-fill returns.

e-Way bills

Section 68 of the CGST Act and Rules 138 to 138E require a person in charge of a conveyance carrying goods beyond a specified value to carry prescribed documents and an electronic way bill. The system tracks movement of goods and supports verification on the road.

Reverse charge mechanism (RCM)

Ordinarily the supplier pays tax. Under Section 9(3) of the CGST Act (and Section 5(3) of the IGST Act), the Government notifies goods and services on which the recipient pays tax. Under Section 9(4), specified registered recipients pay tax on supplies from unregistered persons. RCM shifts the liability where suppliers are numerous, unorganised or outside the tax net.

Departmental notices

Section 61 (scrutiny of returns), Section 65 (departmental audit), Section 66 (special audit), Section 67 (inspection and search), and the demand provisions in Sections 73, 74 and, for FY 2024-25 onwards, Section 74A, together with Rule 142 for show cause notices and orders.

3. Step-by-Step Compliance and Filing Workflow

A. Generating an e-invoice

  1. Raise the invoice in your ERP or billing software with all mandatory fields of the e-invoice schema (INV-01): supplier and recipient GSTINs, document type, number and date, item details with HSN, taxable value, tax rates, and place of supply.
  2. Convert to JSON and upload to an IRP (through the portal, offline utility, API, or a GST Suvidha Provider). Turnover above the notified limit requires two-factor authentication.
  3. The IRP validates the data, checks for duplicates, computes a hash to generate the IRN, digitally signs the invoice and returns the signed JSON with a QR code.
  4. Print the QR code on the invoice. The data flows to GSTR-1 and, if transport details are given, to Part A of the e-way bill.
  5. Report within 30 days of the document date if your turnover is Rs. 10 crore or more.

B. Generating an e-way bill

  1. Log in to ewaybillgst.gov.in (or use the API). Choose Generate New, enter transaction type, document details, item details with HSN and value, and transporter ID or vehicle number.
  2. For e-invoiced documents, generate the e-way bill directly from the IRN.
  3. Hand the e-way bill number to the transporter. Update Part B for change of vehicle or transhipment.
  4. The recipient can reject within 72 hours of generation (or before delivery if earlier); otherwise acceptance is deemed.

C. Discharging reverse charge

  1. Identify RCM transactions at the purchase entry stage through vendor and expense master tagging (advocate, director, GTA, security, rent from unregistered person, import of services).
  2. Determine time of supply: for goods, the earliest of receipt of goods, payment, or 31 days from the supplier's invoice (Section 12(3)); for services, the earlier of payment or 60 days from the supplier's invoice (Section 13(3)).
  3. Where the supplier is unregistered, issue a self-invoice under Section 31(3)(f) within 30 days of receipt of supply (Rule 47A, from 1 November 2024), and a payment voucher at the time of payment.
  4. Report RCM liability in GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d) and pay it in cash only (Section 49(4) prohibits use of ITC for RCM liability).
  5. Claim ITC on the RCM tax paid in Table 4A(2) (imports of services) or 4A(3) (other RCM), subject to Section 16 and 17. Keep the RCM liability and ITC statement on the portal aligned.

D. Responding to ASMT-10 (scrutiny of returns)

  1. Read the discrepancy carefully. Common items: GSTR-1 versus GSTR-3B liability, GSTR-2B versus GSTR-3B ITC, e-way bill values versus returns, and ITC from cancelled suppliers.
  2. Prepare a reconciliation with supporting documents (invoices, credit notes, amendments in later periods, GSTR-9 data).
  3. Reply in Form ASMT-11 on the portal within 30 days of the notice, or within the period allowed by the officer. Where the discrepancy is accepted, pay tax and interest through DRC-03 and mention it in the reply.
  4. If the officer accepts the explanation, he issues ASMT-12 closing the proceeding. If not, he may initiate audit, special audit, inspection or demand proceedings.

E. Responding to a show cause notice (DRC-01)

  1. Check for a prior intimation in DRC-01A under Rule 142(1A). It gives an opportunity to pay or object before the notice is issued.
  2. On receiving the notice and its summary in DRC-01, check the jurisdiction, limitation (is the notice within the time limit?), the computation, and whether the right section (73, 74 or 74A) has been invoked.
  3. File the reply in DRC-06 within the time stated (usually 30 days), with documents and legal submissions. Request a personal hearing; under Section 75(4), it must be granted where requested or where an adverse decision is contemplated. Not more than three adjournments are allowed under Section 75(5).
  4. The order is issued in DRC-07. If aggrieved, appeal under Section 107 within three months, with the pre-deposit.

6. Practical Audit Pitfalls and Best Practices

  • Check e-invoice applicability every April. The test is aggregate turnover in any preceding financial year from 2017-18. Once crossed, the obligation continues even if turnover later falls.
  • Configure the 30-day block in your ERP. Prevent invoices from being backdated or held, and run a daily exception report of documents nearing 30 days.
  • Train dispatch staff on e-way bills. Most detentions arise from expired validity, a missing Part B, or a vehicle change not updated. Keep the transporter's contact in the dispatch file.
  • Build an RCM register. Map every vendor master to forward charge or RCM. Directors' sitting fees, legal fees and rent paid to unregistered landlords are the most frequent misses in audits.
  • Pay RCM in cash, then take credit. Using ITC to pay RCM liability leads to a demand even though the net effect is revenue neutral.
  • Treat every notice as a deadline. Notices appear under "View Additional Notices and Orders" on the portal, not only by email. Check it weekly.
  • Argue limitation first. Many demands are time-barred or invoke Section 74 without evidence of fraud. A reasoned objection on jurisdiction and limitation can dispose of the case.
  • Keep a litigation tracker with notice dates, reply dates, hearing dates, pre-deposits and appeal deadlines.
