EPF, ESIC, Professional Tax and Gratuity Compliance Manual: Coverage, Contribution Rates, ECR Due Dates, PT Slabs and the 15/26 Gratuity Formula

Authored by VipinPublished 14 Jul 2026

A payroll compliance manual for employers in FY 2026-27: provident fund and pension coverage and the 12% split, ESIC coverage and the 3.25% plus 0.75% rates, State professional tax, and gratuity eligibility, computation and payment.

Read this first: the four Labour Codes are in force. The Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020 and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 were brought into force with effect from 21 November 2025. The Code on Social Security subsumes the EPF Act, the ESI Act, the Payment of Gratuity Act and the Maternity Benefit Act, among others. The existing schemes (the EPF Scheme, 1952, the Employees' Pension Scheme, 1995 and the EDLI Scheme, 1976) and ESIC procedures continue to operate under the Code, and rules framed under the earlier laws continue to the extent they are not inconsistent until replaced by rules under the Codes, which the Centre and the States are notifying in stages. This guide therefore uses the familiar section numbers of the earlier Acts as reference labels and points out where the Codes change the position. Always check the rules notified by your State for FY 2026-27.

1. Statutory Overview and Legislative Intent

India's social security architecture for organised sector employees rests on four pillars: a retirement savings and pension system (EPFO), a health insurance and cash benefits system (ESIC), a lump-sum terminal benefit for long service (gratuity), and a State-level tax on employment (professional tax). The first three were governed by separate central laws for decades and are now consolidated in the Code on Social Security, 2020. Professional tax is levied by States under Article 276 of the Constitution.

"Wages" under the Codes

The Code on Social Security (Section 2(88)) and the Code on Wages (Section 2(y)) define wages to include basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, and exclude items such as HRA, conveyance, statutory bonus, overtime, commission, employer's PF contribution and gratuity. Where the excluded components exceed 50% of total remuneration, the excess is added back to wages. This "50% rule" increases the base for PF (where contributions are on actual wages), gratuity and other wage-linked benefits for many salary structures.

3. Step-by-Step Compliance and Filing Workflow

A. EPF

  1. Registration: register the establishment on the EPFO Unified Portal (or through the Shram Suvidha portal or SPICe+ for new companies) to obtain the establishment code.
  2. Onboard employees: collect Form 11 (declaration), generate or link the Universal Account Number (UAN), and seed Aadhaar, PAN and bank account (KYC). Encourage e-nomination.
  3. Monthly payroll: compute contributions on PF wages, prepare the Electronic Challan cum Return (ECR) text file with member-wise wages and contributions, upload on the Unified Portal, verify, and pay the challan (TRRN) through internet banking by the 15th of the following month.
  4. Exits and claims: update date of exit and reason on the portal; employees file claims (Form 19 for PF, 10C or 10D for pension, 31 for advances) online with UAN.
  5. Records: maintain wage registers, contribution cards and ECR acknowledgements. Annual returns are subsumed into monthly ECR filings.

B. ESIC

  1. Registration: register on the ESIC portal (or via SPICe+ or Shram Suvidha) to obtain the 17-digit employer code.
  2. Register employees within 10 days of joining through e-Pehchan, generating the insured person (IP) number; family details are added for medical benefits.
  3. Monthly contributions: file the monthly contribution online (employee-wise days worked and wages) and pay by the 15th of the following month.
  4. Contribution and benefit periods: contribution periods run from 1 April to 30 September and 1 October to 31 March. An employee covered at the start of a contribution period remains covered for the whole period even if wages rise above the ceiling.
  5. Accidents: report employment injuries in the accident report form within the prescribed time (24 hours for serious injuries), and maintain the accident book.

C. Professional tax (Gujarat and Maharashtra examples)

  1. Obtain the employer registration certificate (RC) for deduction from salaries and the enrolment certificate (EC) for the entity's own PT, on the State commercial tax portal.
  2. Deduct PT from salaries every month according to the slab.
  3. Pay and file returns as per the State's periodicity. In Maharashtra, PTRC returns are monthly where the previous year's liability was Rs. 1 lakh or more and annual (by 31 March) where it was below Rs. 50,000; the PTEC amount for the entity is generally payable by 30 June. Gujarat prescribes its own payment and return schedule on the State portal.

D. Gratuity

  1. Display and notice: display the abstract of the Act or Code provisions, and send the notice of opening (Form A under the Payment of Gratuity (Central) Rules) to the controlling authority within 30 days of applicability.
  2. Nomination: obtain nomination in Form F from each employee within 30 days of completing one year of service.
  3. Funding: provide for gratuity on an actuarial basis under AS 15 or Ind AS 19. Many employers fund through a gratuity trust with an insurer; States may mandate insurance under Section 4A of the Payment of Gratuity Act.
  4. Computation on exit: Gratuity = Last drawn wages x 15 / 26 x Completed years of service. A part year of more than six months counts as a full year. For seasonal establishments, 7 days' wages per season; for piece-rated employees, average wages of the last three months.
  5. Payment: pay within 30 days from the date it becomes payable. Issue a written notice of the amount (Form L) and pay through banking channels.
Worked example: an employee resigns after 7 years and 8 months of service with last drawn basic plus DA of Rs. 52,000 per month. Completed years = 8 (the 8 months exceed six months). Gratuity = 52,000 x 15 / 26 x 8 = Rs. 2,40,000. It is within the Rs. 20 lakh ceiling and, for a covered employee, exempt from income tax up to the statutory limit.

6. Practical Audit Pitfalls and Best Practices

  • Test salary structures against the 50% rule. If allowances excluded from wages exceed half of total pay, PF and gratuity costs rise. Model the impact and communicate changes to employees.
  • Do not split basic pay artificially. Following the Vivekananda Vidyamandir judgment, universally paid allowances are part of basic wages for PF.
  • Pay employee contributions before the 15th. One day's delay makes the employee share permanently non-deductible for income tax.
  • Reconcile ECR and ESIC data with payroll every month. Mismatched employee counts are a common inspection trigger.
  • Cover contract workers. Principal employers are liable for PF and ESI of contractors' employees if the contractor defaults. Obtain monthly challans from contractors and verify them on the portals.
  • Seed Aadhaar and KYC early. Unseeded UANs delay claims and transfers and generate employee grievances.
  • Track gratuity eligibility dates including the one-year rule for fixed-term employees under the Code.
  • Watch State notifications on PT slab changes and on the State rules under the Labour Codes.
