SEBI Takeover Code, SME IPOs and SCORES 2.0: SAST Regulations 29, 30 and 31, SME Exchange Eligibility and the 21-Day Grievance Workflow

Authored by VipinPublished 20 Oct 2026

Three areas every growing company meets: substantial shareholding disclosures and open offer triggers under the Takeover Code, the eligibility and post-listing life of an SME IPO, and the investor complaint machinery on SCORES 2.0.

Why these three belong together: they describe the life cycle of an emerging listed company. It raises capital on the SME platform, its promoters and investors cross shareholding thresholds as it grows, and every complaint from its new public shareholders is tracked by SEBI on SCORES.

1. Statutory Overview and Legislative Intent

SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (SAST)

The Takeover Code ensures that when control of a listed company changes, or a person builds a substantial stake, public shareholders get information in time and an exit opportunity at a fair price. Its main components are:

  • Regulation 3(1): an acquirer who, with persons acting in concert (PAC), acquires shares or voting rights entitling them to exercise 25% or more of voting rights must make a public announcement of an open offer.
  • Regulation 3(2): an acquirer already holding 25% or more but less than the maximum permissible non-public shareholding cannot acquire more than 5% of voting rights in any financial year without an open offer (the creeping acquisition limit).
  • Regulation 4: acquisition of control, irrespective of shareholding, triggers an open offer.
  • Regulation 7: the open offer must be for at least 26% of the total shares of the target company.
  • Regulation 10: exemptions, including inter se transfers among qualifying promoters, with prior and post-acquisition reporting.
  • Chapter V (Regulations 29 to 31): disclosure of shareholding and encumbrances.

SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018 (ICDR), Chapter IX

Chapter IX governs initial public offers by small and medium enterprises on the SME platforms of BSE and NSE (BSE SME and NSE Emerge). The intent is to give small businesses access to equity capital with lighter costs, while protecting retail investors through higher minimum application sizes, mandatory underwriting and market making. SEBI tightened the SME framework through the ICDR amendments notified in March 2025 after concerns about inflated issues and misuse of proceeds.

SCORES 2.0 and investor grievance redressal

SCORES is SEBI's online platform for complaints against listed companies and registered intermediaries. Under the SEBI circular of 20 September 2023 on redressal of investor grievances through SCORES and linking it with Online Dispute Resolution, the system was redesigned with strict timelines, auto-routing and auto-escalation. The upgraded SCORES 2.0 platform went live in 2024.

3. Step-by-Step Compliance and Filing Workflow

A. Filing a SAST disclosure

  1. Track the combined holding of the acquirer and PAC after every trade or allotment, including warrants and convertible securities on a fully diluted basis where relevant.
  2. When a threshold is crossed, prepare the disclosure in the format specified by SEBI (separate formats for Regulation 29(1), 29(2) and 31).
  3. Send the disclosure to the target company and file it on the listing portals of every exchange where the shares are listed, within two working days (or seven for encumbrances).
  4. The target company also places the disclosure before the board and keeps it on record. Exchanges publish it, and depositories cross-check promoter encumbrance data.

B. Open offer, in outline

  1. Appoint a SEBI-registered merchant banker as manager to the offer and create an escrow account (at least 25% of consideration for the first Rs. 500 crore and 10% thereafter).
  2. Make the public announcement on the trigger date, publish the detailed public statement within five working days, and file the draft letter of offer with SEBI.
  3. Price the offer at the highest of the parameters in Regulation 8 (negotiated price, volume-weighted average price paid by the acquirer in the previous 52 weeks, highest price paid in 26 weeks, and 60-day volume-weighted market price for frequently traded shares).
  4. Dispatch the letter of offer, run the tendering period of ten working days, and pay shareholders within ten working days of closure of the tendering period.

C. Taking a company to the SME exchange

  1. Readiness review: convert to a public company, align the Articles, restate three years of audited financials (peer reviewed auditor), complete statutory filings, appoint independent directors and a company secretary, and dematerialise all shares including promoter holdings.
  2. Appoint intermediaries: lead manager (merchant banker), registrar to the issue, legal counsel, market maker, underwriter and banker to the issue.
  3. Draft offer document: prepare the draft red herring prospectus or draft prospectus under ICDR and file it with the SME exchange for in-principle approval. The draft is placed in the public domain for comments for the period specified in ICDR before final observations are issued.
  4. Exchange approval and RoC filing: obtain in-principle approval, file the red herring prospectus with the Registrar of Companies, and open the issue (minimum three working days).
  5. Allotment and listing: finalise the basis of allotment, ensure at least 200 allottees, credit shares and list within the T+3 timeline.
  6. Post-listing: comply with LODR as applicable to SME entities (half-yearly results under Regulation 33 within 45 days; corporate governance provisions under Regulations 17 to 27 are relaxed under Regulation 15(2)), and report deviations in use of proceeds.

D. Migration from SME to main board

  1. Under ICDR Regulation 277, an SME-listed company may migrate if its shareholders approve by special resolution through postal ballot, with votes in favour at least twice the votes against, and it meets the main board eligibility criteria and the exchange's migration norms.
  2. Where a further issue would increase post-issue paid-up capital beyond Rs. 25 crore, the issuer must comply with the main board requirements.

E. Handling a SCORES complaint as a listed entity

  1. Register on SCORES, keep the nodal officer's credentials current, and configure email alerts.
  2. On receipt, acknowledge, investigate with the RTA (for issues such as dividend, transfer, transmission or duplicate certificates) and correspond with the investor.
  3. Upload a complete Action Taken Report with supporting documents within 21 calendar days. A generic response is treated as non-redressal.
  4. Track first and second reviews and respond to the designated body and SEBI within their timelines.
  5. Report the quarterly statistics under LODR Regulation 13(3).

6. Practical Audit Pitfalls and Best Practices

  • Identify persons acting in concert broadly. Family members, group companies and parties with common objectives may be PAC even without a formal agreement. Aggregate their holdings before testing thresholds.
  • Count indirect acquisitions. Acquiring control of a holding company can trigger an open offer for the listed subsidiary under Regulation 5.
  • Watch encumbrance definitions. Non-disposal undertakings and similar arrangements are treated as encumbrances under Regulation 28(3).
  • SME use of proceeds is under the microscope. Track deployment against the objects stated in the offer document, report deviations, and avoid any flow of money back to promoters or related parties.
  • Plan market maker spreads and inventory. Thinly traded SME stocks attract surveillance measures; good disclosures and investor relations help maintain liquidity.
  • Keep SCORES responses specific. Attach evidence such as dividend payment proofs, transmission documents or RTA confirmations. Generic replies trigger escalation and count as unresolved.
  • Prevent complaints at source: keep the IEPF transfer process, unclaimed dividend reminders and KYC updation drives running. Most investor complaints relate to dividends and share transmission.

Where the holder and persons acting in concert cross 25% at any point, Regulation 3(1) is triggered and the open offer process starts immediately.
