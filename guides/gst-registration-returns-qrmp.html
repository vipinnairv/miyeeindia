GST Registration, Return Architecture and the QRMP Scheme: Thresholds, Section 24, GSTR-1, IFF, GSTR-3B, GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C

Authored by VipinPublished 22 Sept 2026

Everything a business needs to get on to the GST system and stay compliant through FY 2026-27: registration thresholds and compulsory cases, Aadhaar and biometric authentication, the monthly and quarterly return cycle, annual return slabs, late fees and the three-year time bar.

Context for FY 2026-27: GST rates were rationalised following the 56th GST Council meeting, with a predominantly two-rate structure (5% and 18%) and a higher rate for specified goods, effective from 22 September 2025. The registration and return architecture explained here continues to apply; it is the backbone on which those rates are reported.

1. Statutory Overview and Legislative Intent

GST is a destination-based tax on supply, levied concurrently by the Centre (CGST) and the States (SGST) or Union Territories (UTGST) on intra-State supplies, and by the Centre as IGST on inter-State supplies and imports. Registration is the gateway: only a registered person can collect tax, issue tax invoices and pass on input tax credit. Returns are the mechanism by which the tax collected is reported and the credit chain is validated.

  • Section 22, CGST Act, 2017: every supplier whose aggregate turnover in a financial year exceeds the threshold must register in the State or UT from which taxable supplies are made.
  • Section 23: persons not liable to register (for example those dealing exclusively in exempt or non-taxable supplies, and agriculturists for produce from cultivation).
  • Section 24: categories that must register irrespective of turnover.
  • Section 25: procedure for registration, separate registration for each State and optional separate registrations for business verticals; voluntary registration.
  • Section 2(6): aggregate turnover means the value of all taxable supplies (excluding inward supplies on reverse charge), exempt supplies, exports and inter-State supplies of persons having the same PAN, computed on an all-India basis, excluding taxes.
  • Sections 37, 39 and 44: outward supply statement (GSTR-1), summary return with tax payment (GSTR-3B) and annual return (GSTR-9), with the reconciliation statement (GSTR-9C) under Section 44 read with Rule 80.
  • CGST Rules, 2017: Rules 8 to 26 (registration), Rule 59 (GSTR-1 and IFF), Rule 61 (GSTR-3B and QRMP), Rule 61A (option for quarterly return), Rule 80 (annual return and reconciliation statement).

3. Step-by-Step Compliance and Filing Workflow

A. Obtaining registration

  1. Part A of REG-01: on gst.gov.in, go to Services, then Registration, then New Registration. Enter legal name as per PAN, PAN, State, mobile number and email. Verify both through OTP to obtain a Temporary Reference Number (TRN).
  2. Part B: log in with the TRN and complete business details, promoters or partners, authorised signatory, principal place of business, additional places, goods and services (HSN and SAC), bank account (or later within 30 days under Rule 10A), and State-specific information.
  3. Documents: constitution document (partnership deed, certificate of incorporation), photographs, proof of principal place of business (ownership document, rent agreement with consent letter, electricity bill), and authorisation for the signatory.
  4. Aadhaar authentication: opt for Aadhaar authentication for the promoters and authorised signatory. Under Rule 8(4A), applicants identified on the basis of data analytics and risk parameters must undergo biometric-based Aadhaar authentication and have their photograph taken, along with verification of original documents, at a designated GST Suvidha Kendra.
  5. Verification and submission: sign with DSC (mandatory for companies and LLPs) or EVC. An Application Reference Number (ARN) is generated.
  6. Processing: the officer may seek clarification in REG-03, to which the applicant replies in REG-04 within seven working days. Where Aadhaar authentication is completed and no physical verification is required, the application is deemed approved if not acted upon within seven working days. Where physical verification is required or Aadhaar authentication is not done, the period extends to 30 days.
  7. Certificate: the registration certificate is issued in REG-06 with the 15-character GSTIN. Display it at the principal and every additional place of business (Rule 18).
Simplified registration for small suppliers: the CGST Rules were amended in 2025 to introduce Rule 14A, an optional simplified registration for applicants whose output tax liability on supplies to registered persons does not exceed Rs. 2.5 lakh per month, with quicker electronic approval. Persons under this option must remain within the limit or withdraw from it.

B. Monthly cycle for a regular taxpayer

  1. Invoices and e-invoices: issue tax invoices under Section 31 and Rule 46. E-invoicing taxpayers obtain an IRN for every B2B invoice, and the details flow into GSTR-1 automatically.
  2. GSTR-1 by the 11th: report B2B invoices (invoice-wise), B2C large inter-State invoices above the specified value, B2C others (rate-wise and State-wise), exports, credit and debit notes, amendments, advances, nil-rated and exempt supplies, HSN-wise summary (Table 12, with HSN digits as per turnover) and documents issued (Table 13). Use GSTR-1A to correct before GSTR-3B is filed, where needed.
  3. Invoice Management System (IMS): review inward invoices uploaded by suppliers, and accept, reject or keep them pending. GSTR-2B for the month is generated from IMS actions.
  4. GSTR-2B on the 14th: download and reconcile with the purchase register.
  5. GSTR-3B by the 20th: the system auto-populates outward liability from GSTR-1 and ITC from GSTR-2B. Report outward supplies (Table 3.1), inter-State supplies to unregistered persons (3.2), ITC availed and reversed (Table 4), exempt inward supplies (Table 5) and interest and late fee (5.1). Offset liability using the electronic credit ledger in the order prescribed by Section 49 and Rule 88A, pay the balance in cash through PMT-06, and file with DSC or EVC.

C. Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) scheme

  1. Eligibility: registered persons (other than composition taxpayers) with aggregate turnover up to Rs. 5 crore in the preceding financial year, or new registrants expecting turnover within the limit.
  2. Opting in: select the quarterly option on the portal from the first day of the second month of the preceding quarter to the last day of the first month of the quarter. The option continues until changed.
  3. Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF): optionally upload B2B invoices for the first and second months of the quarter by the 13th of the following month (value of invoices up to Rs. 50 lakh per month), so that customers get credit in their GSTR-2B.
  4. Monthly payment by the 25th for the first two months through PMT-06, using either the Fixed Sum Method (system-computed: 35% of the cash tax paid in the last quarter, or the tax paid in cash in the last month where the preceding quarter was filed monthly) or the Self-Assessment Method (actual liability less ITC). No payment is needed if the balance in the ledgers covers the liability, or if liability is nil.
  5. Quarterly GSTR-1 by the 13th of the month after the quarter, and quarterly GSTR-3B by the 22nd or 24th of that month depending on the State.

D. Annual return and reconciliation

  1. Prepare GSTR-9 by aggregating outward supplies, ITC (with reconciliation against GSTR-2B and books), tax paid and amendments reported in the following year up to the cut-off. Use the auto-drafted values as a starting point only.
  2. Taxpayers with aggregate turnover above Rs. 5 crore also file GSTR-9C, a self-certified reconciliation between audited financial statements and GSTR-9 (certification by a Chartered Accountant or Cost Accountant was removed by the Finance Act, 2021 and Notification No. 30/2021-Central Tax).
  3. Pay any additional liability through DRC-03 with interest before filing.

Three-year time bar: the Finance Act, 2023 amended Sections 37, 39, 44 and 52 so that a return cannot be filed after three years from its due date. The GST portal has enforced this restriction since 2025. Old returns that are not filed can no longer be regularised; the only route left is departmental proceedings.

6. Practical Audit Pitfalls and Best Practices

  • Reconcile GSTR-1 with GSTR-3B every month. Under Rule 88C, if liability in GSTR-1 exceeds GSTR-3B beyond the prescribed limit, the system issues DRC-01B and blocks the next GSTR-1 until the difference is paid or explained.
  • Reconcile e-invoice and e-way bill data with GSTR-1. Department analytics compare these three sources automatically.
  • Get HSN reporting right. Table 12 now uses a validated drop-down of HSN codes; mismatches with invoice data are flagged.
  • Keep registration data current. Amend core fields (address, partners, directors) through REG-14 within 15 days of change. An unregistered additional place of business is a frequent audit observation.
  • Track turnover across all registrations. Aggregate turnover is PAN-wide; QRMP eligibility, e-invoicing, HSN digits and GSTR-9C applicability all depend on it.
  • File nil returns too. A nil return missed for months triggers cancellation and blocks e-way bills.
  • Maintain records for 72 months from the due date of the annual return under Section 35 and Section 36, or longer if proceedings are pending.
