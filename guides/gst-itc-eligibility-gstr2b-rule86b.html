Input Tax Credit: Section 16 Conditions, GSTR-2B Matching, Section 17(5) Blocked Credits and the Rule 86B Cash Payment Restriction

Authored by VipinPublished 29 Sept 2026

The complete ITC playbook: entitlement conditions including the 180-day payment rule and time limit, the IMS and GSTR-2B workflow, reversals under Rules 37, 37A, 42 and 43, blocked credits after the Safari Retreats judgment and the 2025 amendment, and the 1% cash mandate.

Principle: input tax credit is not an automatic right. It is a statutory entitlement that exists only when every condition in Section 16 is satisfied and no restriction in Section 17 applies. Most GST disputes in India are about ITC.

1. Statutory Overview and Legislative Intent

ITC removes the cascading effect of tax: tax paid on inputs, input services and capital goods used in the course or furtherance of business can be set off against output tax. Chapter V of the CGST Act (Sections 16 to 21) and Chapter V of the CGST Rules (Rules 36 to 45) govern eligibility, apportionment, reversal and utilisation. Since 2022 the law has moved firmly towards matching: credit is available only when the supplier has reported the invoice and paid the tax, as reflected in the recipient's GSTR-2B.

Section 16(2): conditions for taking ITC

  1. (a) Document: the registered person is in possession of a tax invoice or debit note issued by a registered supplier, or other prescribed tax-paying documents (for example a bill of entry for imports).
  2. (aa) Reporting by supplier: the details of the invoice or debit note have been furnished by the supplier in GSTR-1 or IFF and communicated to the recipient in GSTR-2B under Section 38.
  3. (b) Receipt: the goods or services have been received. Goods delivered to a third party on the direction of the registered person (bill to ship to) are deemed received. Where goods are received in lots, credit is available on receipt of the last lot.
  4. (ba) No restriction in GSTR-2B: the details are not restricted under Section 38(2)(b) (for example, supplies by newly registered suppliers or defaulting suppliers flagged by the system).
  5. (c) Tax paid by supplier: the tax charged has actually been paid to the Government, in cash or through ITC.
  6. (d) Return filed: the registered person has furnished the return under Section 39.

These are commonly summarised as the four classic conditions (document, receipt, tax paid, return filed), now supplemented by the two matching conditions (aa) and (ba).

The 180-day payment rule (second proviso to Section 16(2))

Where the recipient fails to pay the supplier the value of supply plus tax within 180 days from the date of invoice, ITC availed must be paid back with interest. Under Rule 37 as amended in 2022, the amount of ITC proportionate to the unpaid value is reversed in GSTR-3B for the tax period immediately following the 180 days. The credit can be re-availed when payment is made. The rule does not apply to supplies on reverse charge and to deemed supplies without consideration in Schedule I.

The time limit (Section 16(4))

ITC on an invoice or debit note cannot be taken after 30 November following the end of the financial year to which the invoice relates, or the date of furnishing the annual return, whichever is earlier. Relief provisions were inserted by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 as Section 16(5) (for FY 2017-18 to 2020-21, credit taken in returns filed up to 30 November 2021) and Section 16(6) (on revocation of cancelled registration).

Safari Retreats and the 2025 retrospective amendment. In Chief Commissioner of CGST v. Safari Retreats Pvt. Ltd. (Supreme Court, 3 October 2024), the Court held that the expression "plant or machinery" in Section 17(5)(d) is different from "plant and machinery", and that whether a building qualifies as a "plant" must be decided on a functionality test case by case. The Finance Act, 2025 then amended Section 17(5)(d) retrospectively from 1 July 2017 to read "plant and machinery", aligning it with the definition in the Explanation to Section 17. Taxpayers who had relied on the judgment for buildings such as malls or warehouses should reassess their positions in light of the amendment.

3. Step-by-Step Compliance and Filing Workflow

A. Monthly ITC reconciliation cycle

  1. Day 1 to 10, vendor discipline: remind key suppliers to file GSTR-1 by the 11th (or IFF by the 13th) with correct GSTIN, invoice number, date and tax values.
  2. Day 11 to 13, IMS review: in the Invoice Management System, accept invoices that match the purchase register, reject invoices that are wrong or not received, and keep pending those awaiting receipt (for example goods in transit). Remember that accepting or keeping pending does not by itself establish eligibility; Section 16 and 17 still apply.
  3. Day 14, GSTR-2B: download GSTR-2B in Excel or JSON. It contains ITC available, ITC not available (for example time-barred invoices or place of supply mismatches), and ITC to be reversed (credit notes).
  4. Reconcile at invoice level: match supplier GSTIN, invoice number (normalise prefixes and leading zeros), date, taxable value and each tax head. Classify each line as matched, value mismatch, available in books but not in GSTR-2B, or available in GSTR-2B but not in books.
  5. Apply eligibility filters: remove blocked credits under Section 17(5), personal use, items not yet received, and invoices older than the Section 16(4) limit.
  6. Apportion common credits under Rule 42 (inputs and input services) and Rule 43 (capital goods) where the business makes both taxable and exempt supplies, with the annual true-up by the due date of the September return of the following year.
  7. Fill GSTR-3B Table 4: Table 4A (all ITC available as per GSTR-2B, with imports and reverse charge separately), 4B(1) reversals that are permanent (Rule 38, 42, 43 and Section 17(5)), 4B(2) reversals that may be reclaimed (Rule 37, 37A, invoices not received), 4C net ITC, and 4D(1) ITC reclaimed in this period that was earlier reversed under 4B(2). Track the reclaimable balance in the Electronic Credit Reversal and Re-claimed Statement on the portal.
  8. Utilise credit in the order under Section 49(5) and Rule 88A: IGST credit first against IGST, then against CGST and SGST in any order; CGST credit cannot be used for SGST and vice versa.

B. Year-end and periodic controls

  1. 180-day tracker: from accounts payable ageing, list invoices unpaid beyond 180 days, reverse proportionate ITC in Table 4B(2) with interest under Section 50(3) where the credit had been utilised, and reclaim on payment in Table 4D(1).
  2. Rule 37A: if a supplier has not filed GSTR-3B for the period in which the invoice was reported, by 30 September of the following year, reverse that credit by 30 November and reclaim when the supplier files.
  3. Section 16(4) cut-off: by 30 November, take all pending eligible credits of the previous year, including those of late-filing vendors.
  4. Rule 88D alerts: if ITC claimed in GSTR-3B exceeds GSTR-2B by more than the threshold notified (for example Rs. 25 lakh and 20%), the system issues DRC-01C; reply within seven days by reversing excess or explaining the difference.
  5. Rule 86B check: each month, compute taxable supplies; if above Rs. 50 lakh and no exemption applies, pay at least 1% of output tax in cash.

6. Practical Audit Pitfalls and Best Practices

  • Never claim on the purchase register alone. Since 1 January 2022, Rule 36(4) and Section 16(2)(aa) allow ITC only to the extent it appears in GSTR-2B. Provisional credit is no longer permitted.
  • Watch place of supply mismatches. A hotel in another State charging that State's CGST and SGST will appear in GSTR-2B as ineligible for you. Ensure the supplier charges IGST where your registration is in a different State, or register in that State.
  • Document receipt. Keep goods receipt notes, transporter documents and e-way bills. For services, keep contracts, deliverables and acceptance emails. Departmental audits frequently challenge receipt.
  • Vendor due diligence. Check the GST status of new vendors before the first payment. Cancelled or suspended GSTINs, and suppliers who file GSTR-1 but not GSTR-3B, are the main source of later demands.
  • Separate employee-related costs. Food, health insurance, club memberships and leave travel need a classification at the expense ledger level so that blocked credits are not claimed by default.
  • Capital work in progress review. For construction projects, segregate plant and machinery from building costs at the purchase order stage to avoid blocked credit disputes under Section 17(5)(c) and (d).
  • Reconcile GSTR-9 Table 8. The difference between GSTR-2B values and ITC claimed is examined closely in scrutiny under Section 61. Prepare a reasoned reconciliation in advance.
